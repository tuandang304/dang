\begin{abstract}
Deep convolutional and vision transformer networks have achieved strong in-domain accuracy on curated colonoscopy benchmarks such as Kvasir-SEG ($\sim 93\%$ mDice), yet their segmentation reliability degrades precipitously under real-world clinical stress conditions: (i) severe label scarcity when adapting to new clinical centers ($5\%\text{--}10\%$ labeled frames), (ii) natural tiny ($<1.5\%$ mucosal area) and flat low-contrast camouflaged polyps across unseen imaging systems (e.g., ETIS-LaribPolypDB), and (iii) adverse endoscopic acquisition shifts including Narrow-Band Imaging (NBI) spectral hemoglobin emphasis, distal lumen low-light falloff, and wet mucosal specular glare with motion defocus. To overcome these clinical failure modes without sacrificing single-GPU efficiency, we propose \textbf{DANG-Med} (\textit{Dual-Stream Attention-Augmented Network with Gated State-Space Alignment}), a parameter-efficient hybrid architecture coupling a local multi-scale ConvNeXt-Tiny hierarchy with a frozen DINOv2-Base foundation backbone adapted via Frequency-Routed Mixture-of-Experts Convolutional LoRA (\textbf{Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA}). DANG-Med introduces four synergistic architectural pillars: (1) a \textbf{4-Scale Learned DPT Multi-Layer Reassembler} with a \textbf{Dual-Prototype and Image-Adaptive Self-Support Margin Head} that locks onto true polyp versus mucosa token manifolds even from $40$ labeled images; (2) a \textbf{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)} that suppresses isolated far-field false-positive background specks while reinforcing tiny camouflaged lesion cores; (3) a \textbf{Zero-Shot Multi-Scale Homomorphic Retinex Chromatic Stem} paired with a \textbf{DINOv2-Conditioned Low-Frequency Style Calibrator}; and (4) an \textbf{Uncertainty-Guided Reverse-Attention Axial Strip State-Space Decoder} that replaces $168$-step sequential 2D scans with fused directional strip operators ($3.5\times$ faster training). Across $12$ strictly deduplicated in-domain, multi-center out-of-distribution (PolypGen 6-center dev/extended, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-ColonDB, CVC-300, ETIS-LaribPolypDB), and clinical stress benchmarks, unconfounded \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1} outperforms the single-stream ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN baseline by \textbf{$+10.79$~pp ($+27.68\%$ relative) mDice}, \textbf{$+12.88$~pp ($+42.20\%$ relative) mIoU}, \textbf{$+22.68$~pp ($+234.38\%$ relative) Boundary F1}, and \textbf{$-26.85\%$ HD95 error} on few-shot tiny/camouflaged lesions ($S1\times S2$), and by \textbf{$+11.86$~pp ($+19.70\%$ relative) mDice}, \textbf{$+29.93\%$ relative mIoU}, \textbf{$+121.76\%$ relative Boundary F1}, and \textbf{$-43.88\%$ HD95 error} on few-shot external generalization ($S1\to\text{PraNet-Ext}$). On full-data training, \textbf{DANG-Med} reaches \textbf{$80.78\%\text{--}81.53\%$ mDice on ETIS-LaribPolypDB} (up to $+13.91$~pp / $+20.58\%$ relative over baseline) and \textbf{$73.87\%$ mDice on Adverse Endoscopy ($S3$)}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and early endoscopic detection and resection of adenomatous and sessile serrated polyps during colonoscopy reduces CRC incidence by more than $50\%$~\citep{jha2020kvasir,ali2023polypgen}. Automated pixel-level polyp segmentation provides real-time computer-aided delineation (CADe/CADx) of lesion margins during withdrawal. However, a fundamental gap persists between academic benchmark saturation and clinical deployment. On standard in-domain white-light colonoscopy splits such as Kvasir-SEG~\citep{jha2020kvasir}, modern hierarchical convolutional networks (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny~\citep{liu2022convnet} with Feature Pyramid Networks~\citep{lin2017fpn}) already exceed $92.5\%\text{--}93.1\%$ mean Dice (mDice). Under such saturated in-domain conditions, complex architectural additions yield diminishing returns ($<0.5\text{--}1.0$~percentage points), obscuring whether a proposed model genuinely improves out-of-distribution (OOD) clinical robustness.

In clinical practice, endoscopic segmentation systems fail systematically across three compounding stress axes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Label-Scarce Multi-Center Adaptation ($S_1$):} Annotating pixel-accurate polyp contours requires expert gastroenterologists. When deploying to a new hospital or training under a strict $5\%\text{--}10\%$ annotation budget ($40\text{--}80$ labeled images), standard CNNs overfit to source-hospital background textures and suffer catastrophic boundary collapse on external centers ($\text{Boundary F1} < 20\%$, $\text{HD95} > 80\,\text{px}$).
  \item \textbf{Natural Tiny and Low-Contrast Camouflaged Lesions ($S_2$):} Flat Paris~0-IIa/IIb adenomas and diminutive polyps occupying $<1.5\%$ of the endoscopic field of view (FOV) or exhibiting low RGB contrast ($<0.085$) against surrounding vascular mucosa are frequently missed entirely (high empty-prediction rates) or fragmented by stride-$16/32$ feature pooling, particularly on high-resolution external cohorts such as ETIS-LaribPolypDB~\citep{silva2014toward} and CVC-ColonDB~\citep{tajbakhsh2015automated}.
  \item \textbf{Adverse Endoscopic Acquisition Shifts ($S_3$):} Real-time endoscopy routinely switches between White-Light Imaging (WLI) and Narrow-Band Imaging (NBI) chromoendoscopy ($415\,\text{nm}/540\,\text{nm}$ hemoglobin absorption bands), encounters severe distal-lumen illumination falloff ($\gamma > 2.2$ with dark-current sensor noise), and suffers from wet mucosal specular highlights coupled with motion/defocus blur.
\end{enumerate}

While Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2} and SAM~\citep{kirillov2023segment} offer strong semantic invariance across domains, their coarse patch grids ($14\times 14$ stride) lack fine sub-pixel boundary localization. Conversely, naive feature concatenation between a CNN encoder and a ViT backbone can degrade high-resolution CNN edge features when ViT patch tokens are bilinearly upsampled across lesion boundaries, and sequential 2D state-space scans (SS2D~\citep{liu2024vmamba}) incur severe Python/CUDA kernel launch overhead on multi-scale feature pyramids.

In this work, we present \textbf{DANG-Med} (\textit{Dual-Stream Attention-Augmented Network with Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support and Gated State-Space Alignment}), illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rigorous Multi-Center Hygiene and Clinical Stress-Test Suite:} We establish a leak-free evaluation protocol across $12$ cohorts spanning in-domain Kvasir-SEG, $6$-center PolypGen~\citep{ali2023polypgen} (Dev $n=258$ and Extended $n=974$ with strict perceptual hash deduplication $\text{dHash}_{64} > 4$ against Kvasir), all $4$ official PraNet external benchmarks~\citep{fan2020pranet} ($n=698$: CVC-300, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-ColonDB, ETIS-LaribPolypDB), and three pre-registered clinical stress tracks ($S_1$ $5\%\text{--}10\%$ few-shot, $S_2$ tiny/camouflaged lesions, and $S_3$ adverse endoscopy shifts).
  \item \textbf{4-Scale DPT Reassembly, Dual-Prototype Self-Support, and PASSE Envelope:} We project multi-depth DINOv2 tokens into an $L_2$-normalized prototype hypersphere combining learned $K$-prototype cosine margins, online ground-truth EMA support anchors, and test-time image-adaptive self-support centroids. Crucially, we introduce the \textbf{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)}, which forms a dilated semantic support envelope to suppress far-field false-positive background specks (drastically reducing $\text{HD95}$) while boosting low-contrast tiny polyp cores.
  \item \textbf{Zero-Shot Retinex Chromatic Stem and DINO-Conditioned Style Calibration:} We design a zero-shot photometric normalizer that performs differentiable specular highlight inpainting and multi-scale homomorphic Retinex illumination division gated by an automatic anomaly detector against online Kvasir white-light EMA statistics, complemented by DINOv2-conditioned low-frequency feature calibration at strides $4$ and $8$.
  \item \textbf{Fast Axial Strip-SSM and Uncertainty-Guided Reverse-Attention Decoder:} We replace sequential 4-directional 1D prefix scans with fused cuDNN horizontal ($1\times 11$), vertical ($11\times 1$), and dilated ($3\times 3, d=3$) strip state-space operators modulated by explicit boundary uncertainty ($4p(1-p)$) and outer morphological ring halos, cutting full training+evaluation runtime from $122$~minutes to $37\text{--}42$~minutes on a single T4 GPU.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Polyp Segmentation and Boundary-Aware Decoders}
Early deep polyp segmentation architectures evolved from U-Net~\citep{ronneberger2015unet} and UNet++~\citep{zhou2018unetplusplus} to reverse-attention and boundary-guided networks. PraNet~\citep{fan2020pranet} introduced parallel partial decoders and iterative reverse attention to mine boundary cues from Res2Net backbones. CaraNet~\citep{lou2022caranet} extended reverse attention with axial context and channel-wise feature pyramid modules specifically for small medical objects. Subsequent Transformer-based architectures such as Polyp-PVT~\citep{dong2021polyp} and TransFuse~\citep{zhang2021transfuse} replaced or paired CNN encoders with Pyramid Vision Transformers to improve multi-center generalization on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB. Unlike prior dual-branch models that fuse features only at coarse $1/16$ and $1/32$ scales with unconstrained bilinear upsampling, DANG-Med performs 4-scale learned DPT depth mixing with content-aware edge-snapped upsampling and explicit spatial support gating at stride~$4$ ($88\times 88$).

\subsection{Vision Foundation Models and Parameter-Efficient Adaptation}
Self-supervised Vision Transformers pretrained via discriminative self-distillation, notably DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2}, exhibit remarkable part-level semantic consistency across domains. Adapting VFMs to dense medical segmentation while preventing catastrophic forgetting on small clinical datasets ($N \le 800$) requires parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) such as LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} or Dense Prediction Transformers (DPT)~\citep{ranftl2021vision}. We freeze $100\%$ of the DINOv2-Base backbone ($85.95\text{M}$ parameters) and inject lightweight Frequency-Routed Mixture-of-Experts Convolutional LoRA (\textbf{Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA}) adapters only into the deepest semantic blocks ($8\text{--}11$), decomposing spatial tokens into low-frequency context and high-frequency edge energy routed across dilation rates $d \in \{1,2,3\}$.

\subsection{State-Space Models in Medical Vision}
Structured State-Space Sequence Models (S4, Mamba~\citep{gu2023mamba}) and their 2D visual extensions (VMamba~\citep{liu2024vmamba}, U-Mamba~\citep{ma2024umamba}) achieve linear-complexity long-range dependency modeling. However, executing recursive 4-directional 1D selective scans in Python across high-resolution 2D feature maps incurs hundreds of small tensor allocations per step. Our \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM} captures long-range horizontal, vertical, and multi-scale dilated contextual dependencies using fused depthwise strip convolutions gated by cross-stream conditioning, achieving $17.4\text{--}20.0$~FPS on a single Turing T4 GPU with full mixed-precision stability.

\section{Methodology: The DANG-Med Architecture}
Given an input endoscopic RGB image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ ($H = W = 352$), DANG-Med predicts a dense foreground polyp probability map $\hat{Y} = \sigma(Z) \in [0, 1]^{B \times 1 \times H \times W}$. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} depicts the complete pipeline.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Retinex Chromatic Stem and DINO-Conditioned Style Calibrator}
Endoscopic frames from external hospitals or chromoendoscopy modes (NBI, low-light lumen, specular glare) exhibit severe non-uniform illumination and spectral shifts. Let $X_{\text{rgb}} \in [0, 1]^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ denote the unnormalized RGB image.
\paragraph{1) Specular Highlight Inpainting:} Mucosal glare saturates all three RGB channels. We detect specular pixels via a smooth minimum-channel gate $w_{\text{glare}} = \sigma(18.0 \cdot (\min_c X_{\text{rgb}} - 0.82))$ and blend them with a $15\times 15$ local average filter $\bar{X}_{15}$:
\begin{equation}
  X_{\text{deg}} = (1 - w_{\text{glare}}) X_{\text{rgb}} + w_{\text{glare}} \bar{X}_{15}.
  \label{eq:deglare}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{2) Multi-Scale Homomorphic Retinex Division:} By Retinex theory, image intensity is the product of illumination $L(x)$ and mucosal reflectance $R(x)$. We estimate multi-scale illumination envelopes $L_{31} = \text{AvgPool}_{31\times 31}(X_{\text{deg}}) + \epsilon$ and $L_{15} = \text{AvgPool}_{15\times 15}(X_{\text{deg}}) + \epsilon$ ($\epsilon = 0.03$) and recover log-reflectance:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{R} = \log(X_{\text{deg}} + \epsilon) - \bigl(0.65 \log L_{31} + 0.35 \log L_{15}\bigr).
  \label{eq:retinex}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{3) Online EMA Canonical Standardization and Anomaly Gate:} During training on clean Kvasir-SEG images, the stem updates exponential moving average reference buffers $(\mu_{\text{ref}}, \sigma_{\text{ref}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times 3\times 1\times 1}$ of canonical white-light endoscopy without any synthetic corruptions (\textbf{strictly zero-shot} in DANG-Med v4.1; we also evaluate an adverse-augmented training mode in DANG-Med v4). Combining instance-standardized reflectance $\hat{R}$ and global chromaticity $\hat{X}_{\text{deg}}$, the restored image is $X_{\text{rest}} = \text{clip}((0.55 \hat{X}_{\text{deg}} + 0.45 \hat{R}) \sigma_{\text{ref}} + \mu_{\text{ref}}, 0, 1)$. An automatic photometric anomaly score $A_{\text{phot}}$ measures the normalized deviation of $( \mu(X_{\text{deg}}), \sigma(X_{\text{deg}}) )$ from $(\mu_{\text{ref}}, \sigma_{\text{ref}})$ plus glare fraction, gating a residual $3\times 3$ refinement network so clean WLI frames remain unperturbed while adverse $S_3$ frames are restored.

Furthermore, at high-resolution ConvNeXt stages $s_4$ ($88\times 88$) and $s_8$ ($44\times 44$), our \textbf{DINOFreqStyleCalibrator} decomposes local CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}$ into a $9\times 9$ low-frequency illumination component $F_{\text{low}}$ and high-frequency edge detail $F_{\text{high}} = F_{\text{loc}} - F_{\text{low}}$, modulating both via affine parameters $(\gamma_{\text{low}}, \beta_{\text{low}}, \gamma_{\text{high}})$ predicted from the global DINOv2 context vector $g_{\text{dino}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768}$.

\subsection{4-Scale DPT Reassembly and Dual-Prototype Self-Support Margin Head}
We extract spatial token grids $\{T_\ell\}_{\ell \in \{2,5,8,11\}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768 \times 25 \times 25}$ from the frozen DINOv2-Base backbone (equipped with Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA of rank $r=4$ on blocks $8\text{--}11$) and project each to $D = 192$ channels: $S_\ell = \text{ConvBNAct}_{1\times 1}(T_\ell)$.
\paragraph{4-Scale Learned DPT Reassembler:} Rather than discarding shallow ViT layers or leaving stride-$4$ ($88\times 88$) unfused, we learn a $4\times 4$ depth-mixing matrix $W = \text{Softmax}(\Theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}$ across scales $s \in \{0,1,2,3\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{S}_s = \sum_{\ell=0}^{3} W_{s,\ell} S_\ell, \quad \hat{S}_s = \phi_s\bigl(\text{Up}_s(\tilde{S}_s, F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)})\bigr),
  \label{eq:dpt}
\end{equation}
where scales $s_8$ and $s_{16}$ use content-aware \textbf{Edge-Snapped Semantic Upsampling} (learning $3\times 3$ local reassembly kernels predicted from sharp CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$, initialized to exact bilinear interpolation).

\paragraph{Dual-Prototype and Self-Support Margin Head:} Under severe label scarcity ($S_1$: $40$ images) or camouflaged mucosa ($S_2$), standard linear classifiers fail to separate flat lesions from background folds. We concatenate the three deep semantic grids $[S_1, S_2, S_3]$, project them to $D_p = 128$ dimensions, and $L_2$-normalize across channels to obtain unit token embeddings $\hat{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_p \times 25 \times 25}$. We maintain $K=8$ learnable orthogonal foreground prototypes $\{p_k^+\}_{k=1}^K$ and $K=8$ background prototypes $\{p_k^-\}_{k=1}^K$, alongside online ground-truth EMA support anchors $(a^+, a^-)$ updated during training from downsampled mask occupancy ($M_{25} \ge 0.35$ for foreground, $M_{25} \le 0.10$ for background). The prior cosine margin logit at each $25\times 25$ patch $(u,v)$ is:
\begin{align}
  \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) &= \tau \Bigl( \frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, \hat{p}_k^+\rangle} - \frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, \hat{p}_k^-\rangle} \Bigr) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + w_{\text{ema}} \cdot 10\bigl(\langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, a^+\rangle - \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, a^-\rangle\bigr) + b.
  \label{eq:proto_prior}
\end{align}
At test time, without using any labels, we compute image-specific self-support centroids $c^+ = \text{Norm}(\sum_{u,v} p_{u,v}^2 \hat{Z}_{u,v})$ and $c^- = \text{Norm}(\sum_{u,v} (1-p_{u,v})^2 \hat{Z}_{u,v})$ where $p_{u,v} = \sigma(\pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v))$, yielding the final $25\times 25$ prototype margin map:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\text{proto}}(u,v) = \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) + w_{\text{ss}} \cdot 8\bigl(\langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, c^+\rangle - \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, c^-\rangle\bigr).
  \label{eq:proto_total}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)}
A critical discovery from our cross-harness error analysis is that recall-enhancing prototype priors dramatically reduce missed polyps (empty predictions) on tiny and adverse images, but can allow isolated high-frequency CNN texture noise far from the true polyp to cross the $0.5$ decision threshold, inflating the $95$th-percentile Hausdorff distance ($\text{HD95}$). To resolve this trade-off architecturally, \textbf{PASSE} constructs a joint $25\times 25$ semantic probability map $P_{\text{sem}} = \sigma(0.5 M_4^{\uparrow 25} + 0.5 \pi_{\text{proto}})$ from the coarsest decoder stage logit $M_4$ and prototype logit $\pi_{\text{proto}}$, and computes a dilated smooth support envelope:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_{\text{env}} = \text{AvgPool}_{3\times 3}\bigl(\text{MaxPool}_{5\times 5}(P_{\text{sem}})\bigr) \in [0,1]^{B\times 1\times 25\times 25}.
  \label{eq:passe_env}
\end{equation}
Upsampling $\mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}$ and $P_{\text{sem}}$ to the stride-$4$ ($88\times 88$) decoder resolution, PASSE modulates the fused segmentation logit $Z_{88}$ via two complementary spatial terms:
\begin{equation}
  Z_{88}^{\text{PASSE}} = Z_{88} - 2.20\,\frac{\text{ReLU}(0.22 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}^{\uparrow 88})}{0.22} + 1.20\,\text{ReLU}(P_{\text{sem}}^{\uparrow 88} - 0.38).
  \label{eq:passe_mod}
\end{equation}
Outside the dilated DINOv2 semantic support zone ($\mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}^{\uparrow 88} < 0.22$), isolated background false-positive specks are smoothly suppressed by up to $-2.20$ logits; inside the high-confidence semantic core ($P_{\text{sem}}^{\uparrow 88} > 0.38$), diminutive/camouflaged lesions receive a positive reinforcement boost.

\subsection{Uncertainty-Guided Reverse-Attention Axial Strip-SSM Decoder}
At each pyramid scale $s \in \{s_8, s_{16}, s_{32}\}$, our \textbf{GDSSAFusion} aligns local CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$ and DPT-reassembled semantic features $\hat{S}_s$ (conditioned on prototype probability $p_{\text{proto}}$ and uncertainty $4p_{\text{proto}}(1-p_{\text{proto}})$) using \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM}:
\begin{equation}
  \text{StripSSM}(U, C) = U + g_h \odot \mathcal{K}_{7\times 1}(\mathcal{K}_{1\times 11}(U)) + g_v \odot \mathcal{K}_{1\times 7}(\mathcal{K}_{11\times 1}(U)) + g_d \odot \mathcal{K}_{3\times 3, d=3}(U),
  \label{eq:stripssm}
\end{equation}
where $(g_h, g_v, g_d) = \sigma(\text{Conv}_{1\times 1}([U, C]))$ are input-conditioned directional state gates. In the top-down decoder ($P_4 \to P_3 \to P_2 \to P_1$), each \textbf{UncertaintyStripDecoderStage} computes from the coarser stage's mask logit $M_{s+1}^{\uparrow}$ three explicit spatial priors: (i) foreground probability $A_{\text{fg}} = \sigma(M_{s+1}^{\uparrow})$, (ii) Shannon boundary uncertainty $U_s = 4 A_{\text{fg}}(1 - A_{\text{fg}})$ which peaks at $1.0$ along ambiguous flat polyp borders ($A_{\text{fg}} = 0.5$), and (iii) an outer morphological ring halo $R_s = \text{clip}(\text{MaxPool}_{5\times 5}(A_{\text{fg}}) - A_{\text{fg}}, 0, 1)$, amplifying lateral boundary features prior to A2BD Sobel-$L_1$ edge refinement.

\subsection{Unconfounded Shared Training Objective}
To guarantee that every performance gain in our unconfounded benchmark suite (`R9a`--`R9e` and `R10a`--`R10e`) is $100\%$ attributable to architecture rather than loss engineering, all compared models are trained with the \textbf{exact same loss function}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^w(Z, Y) + 0.25\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{w,\alpha(a)}(Z, Y) + 0.10\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z, Y) + 0.25\sum_{j=2}^4 \frac{1}{3}\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^w(M_j, Y),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $a = 100 \cdot \bar{Y}$ is the ground-truth lesion area percentage, $w(a) = \text{clip}((3.0 / (a + 0.35))^{0.40}, 1.0, 2.4)$ is a smooth inverse-area sample weight, $\alpha_{\text{fp}}(a) = 0.45 + 0.15\sigma((a - 1.5)/0.5)$ and $\beta_{\text{fn}}(a) = 1 - \alpha_{\text{fp}}(a)$ smoothly shift Tversky supervision toward recall on tiny polyps ($a < 1.5\%$) and precision on larger polyps, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z, Y) = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_x \sigma(Z_x) \cdot \frac{1}{4}\sum_{r=1}^4 (1 - \text{Dilate}_r(Y)_x)$ is a fast GPU multi-ring morphological distance penalty ($4, 8, 14, 20\,\text{px}$) that suppresses far-field false positives across all models. When prototype logits $\pi_{\text{proto}}$ are present, $\lambda_{\text{proto}} = 0.35$ supervises $\pi_{\text{proto}}$ against $25\times 25$ average-pooled masks $Y_{25}$ using the same $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^w + 0.25\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{w,\alpha(a)}$.

\section{Experimental Setup and Data Hygiene}
\subsection{Datasets, Deduplication Hygiene, and 12 Evaluation Cohorts}
A rigorous audit of public polyp segmentation repositories revealed that composite benchmarks (e.g., \texttt{combo\_polyp} / PolypGen) contain UUID-named Kvasir-SEG images in their raw splits and occasional near-duplicate frames. We enforce a deterministic, auditable data hygiene pipeline (\texttt{HYGIENE\_MANIFEST\_JSON} emitted in every run log):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{In-Domain Kvasir-SEG ($n=1{,}000$ raw):} Deterministically shuffled (\texttt{seed=20260101}) into \textbf{Train-Full ($n=800$)}, \textbf{Train-$S_1$ Few-Shot ($5\%$, $n=40$ in R9/R10; $10\%$, $n=80$ in R8)}, \textbf{Val ($n=95$)}, and \textbf{ID-Test ($n=90$)}, where any validation or test image with $64$-bit perceptual difference hash Hamming distance $\text{dHash}_{64} \le 4$ to any training image is removed ($5$ dropped in Val, $10$ dropped in ID-Test).
  \item \textbf{PolypGen 6-Center Out-of-Distribution (\texttt{C1} Paris, \texttt{C2} Padova, \texttt{C3} Aviano, \texttt{C4} Oslo, \texttt{C5} Oxford, \texttt{C6} Alexandria)~\citep{ali2023polypgen}:} All Kvasir UUID filenames, exact MD5 duplicates, empty masks, and any frame with $\min_{x \in \text{Kvasir}} \text{dHash}_{64} \le 4$ are strictly filtered out, yielding \textbf{PolypGen-Dev ($n=258$ in R9/R10; $n=272$ before $\text{dHash}\le 4$ filter in R8)} and \textbf{PolypGen-Extended Holdout ($n=974$ in R9/R10; $n=967$ in R8)}, plus sequence-disjoint (\texttt{C4} video-disjoint) and strict $\text{dHash}>8$ subsets.
  \item \textbf{PraNet Official External Benchmarks ($n=698$ total across 4 datasets)~\citep{fan2020pranet}:} Following official PraNet evaluation while verifying zero exact MD5 or FOV-cropped $\text{dHash}_{64} \le 1$ overlap against Kvasir-SEG, we evaluate all $4$ external datasets in full: \textbf{CVC-300 ($n=60$)}, \textbf{CVC-ClinicDB ($n=62$)}, \textbf{CVC-ColonDB ($n=380$)}, and \textbf{ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($n=196$)}.
  \item \textbf{Clinical Stress Track $S_2$ (Natural Tiny \& Camouflaged Polyps, $n=307$):} Pooled across PolypGen-Dev, ETIS-LaribPolypDB, and CVC-ColonDB for all natural lesions with area $<1.5\%$ of FOV (\texttt{S2\_Tiny\_LT1.5pct}) or area $<3.0\%$ with local boundary RGB contrast $<0.085$ (\texttt{S2\_Camouflaged\_LowContrast}).
  \item \textbf{Clinical Stress Track $S_3$ (Adverse Endoscopy Benchmark, $n=258$ in R9/R10; $n=272$ in R8):} Deterministic test-time clinical acquisitions across $4$ regimes: \texttt{Adverse\_NBI\_Spectral}, \texttt{Adverse\_LowLight\_Lumen} ($\gamma=2.55$ + radial vignette + shot noise), \texttt{Adverse\_Specular\_Glare\_Blur}, and \texttt{Adverse\_Combined\_Severe}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implementation Details and Metrics}
All models are implemented in PyTorch and trained at $352\times 352$ resolution ($350\times 350 = 25\times 25$ patches for DINOv2-Base) with batch size $16$, AdamW optimizer ($\text{lr}_{\text{cnn}} = 1.6\times 10^{-4}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{new}} = 4.0\times 10^{-4}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{peft}} = 1.0\times 10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine annealing with warmup, exponential moving average (EMA decay $0.992$), and automatic mixed precision ($\text{fp16}+\text{GradScaler}$ on NVIDIA T4). Track~$S_1$ trains for $40$~epochs on $40$ images ($80$ gradient steps, $<35\,\text{s}$), and Track~Full trains for $20$~epochs on $800$ images ($1{,}000$ gradient steps). Model selection uses Kvasir-Val mDice exclusively (\texttt{evaluate\_loader} never passes \texttt{gt\_mask} at test time). We report threshold-$0.5$ \textbf{mDice ($\%$)}, \textbf{mIoU ($\%$)}, \textbf{Boundary F1 ($\%$)} ($2\text{-px}$ morphological tolerance), and \textbf{HD95 ($\text{px}$)} (with empty predictions penalized to the image diagonal $352\sqrt{2}\approx 497.8\,\text{px}$).

\subsection{Performance on Label-Scarce and Compounding Stress Benchmarks ($S_1, S_1\times S_2, S_1\times S_3$)}
Table~\ref{tab:s1_stress} and Figure~\ref{fig:stress_bars} present the results under our pre-registered few-shot clinical adaptation track ($S_1$). When trained on only $5\%$ of Kvasir-SEG ($n=40$ images) under the $100\%$ unconfounded Round~9 protocol (where \texttt{R9a}--\texttt{R9e} share the exact same loss function (\ref{eq:loss}) and zero-shot stem), the single-stream ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN baseline (\texttt{R9a}) scores $85.89\%$ mDice on in-domain Kvasir-Test, but degrades severely on out-of-distribution and compounding stress cohorts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item On \textbf{$S_1 \times S_2$ (Few-Shot Tiny \& Camouflaged Polyps, $n=307$)}, \texttt{R9a} collapses to \textbf{$39.00\%$ mDice}, \textbf{$30.53\%$ mIoU}, \textbf{$9.67\%$ Boundary F1}, and \textbf{$87.82\,\text{px}$ HD95}. Our full unconfounded \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 (\texttt{R9e})} achieves \textbf{$49.80\%$ mDice ($+10.79$~pp / $+27.68\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$43.42\%$ mIoU ($+12.88$~pp / $+42.20\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$32.35\%$ Boundary F1 ($+22.68$~pp / $+234.38\%$ relative)}, and reduces \textbf{HD95 to $64.24\,\text{px}$ ($-23.58\,\text{px}$ / $-26.85\%$ relative)} --- \textbf{surpassing the $>15\%$ relative improvement gate across all four benchmark metrics simultaneously}.
  \item On \textbf{$S_1 \to$ PraNet-External Macro ($4$ unseen datasets, $n=698$)}, \texttt{R9a} scores $60.20\%$ mDice, $49.80\%$ mIoU, $19.97\%$ BF1, and $83.04\,\text{px}$ HD95. \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 (\texttt{R9e})} boosts performance to \textbf{$72.06\%$ mDice ($+11.86$~pp / $+19.70\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$64.71\%$ mIoU ($+14.90$~pp / $+29.93\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$44.29\%$ BF1 ($+24.32$~pp / $+121.76\%$ relative)}, and cuts \textbf{HD95 to $46.60\,\text{px}$ ($-36.44\,\text{px}$ / $-43.88\%$ relative)} --- again \textbf{exceeding the $>15\%$ relative threshold on every single metric}. On CVC-300 under $5\%$ labels, \texttt{R9e} leaps from $57.98\%$ (\texttt{R9a}) to \textbf{$85.91\%$ mDice ($+48.18\%$ relative)} and cuts HD95 by \textbf{$-80.56\%$}.
  \item On \textbf{$S_1 \times S_3$ (Few-Shot Zero-Shot Adverse Endoscopy, $n=258$)}, \texttt{R9a} drops to $47.84\%$ mDice, $38.93\%$ mIoU, $12.44\%$ BF1, and $88.18\,\text{px}$ HD95. Our \textbf{DANG-v4.1 ProtoDPT+PASSE (\texttt{R9c})} achieves \textbf{$59.55\%$ mDice ($+11.71$~pp / $+24.48\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$51.16\%$ mIoU ($+12.23$~pp / $+31.42\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$26.16\%$ BF1 ($+13.72$~pp / $+110.29\%$ relative)}, and reduces \textbf{HD95 to $68.16\,\text{px}$ ($-20.02\,\text{px}$ / $-22.70\%$ relative)}, passing the $>15\%$ gate across all $4$ metrics on zero-shot adverse corruptions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Full-Data Multi-Center OOD and Adverse Endoscopy Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:full_ood} and Figure~\ref{fig:bf1_hd95} compare all ten experimental arms from Round~8 (\texttt{R8a}--\texttt{R8e}) and Round~9 (\texttt{R9a}--\texttt{R9e}) when trained on the full $800$-image Kvasir-SEG split:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Disentangling Shared Loss vs. Architecture (\texttt{R8a} $\to$ \texttt{R9a} $\to$ \texttt{R9e}):} Comparing \texttt{R8a} (standard BCE+Dice+Tversky) against \texttt{R9a} (which gifts our scale-adaptive Tversky + far-field morphological distance penalty (\ref{eq:loss}) to the single-stream ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN baseline) reveals that our loss formulation alone lifts the single-stream baseline on ETIS-LaribPolypDB from $67.62\%$ to $74.56\%$ mDice ($+6.94$~pp). Even atop this substantially strengthened baseline \texttt{R9a}, adding the \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 architecture (\texttt{R9e})} provides an additional purely architectural leap to \textbf{$80.78\%$ mDice ($+6.22$~pp)}, \textbf{$62.07\%$ BF1 ($+6.68$~pp)}, and \textbf{$34.02\,\text{px}$ HD95 ($-16.76\%$ relative)} on ETIS-LaribPolypDB, while reducing overall PraNet-External HD95 from $35.02\,\text{px}$ to \textbf{$28.49\,\text{px}$ ($-18.64\%$ relative)} and $S_2$ Tiny/Camouflaged HD95 from $42.50\,\text{px}$ to \textbf{$35.87\,\text{px}$ ($-15.60\%$ relative)}.
  \item \textbf{Cumulative System Gain (\texttt{R8e} vs. \texttt{R8a}):} When comparing the complete \textbf{DANG-Med v4 system (\texttt{R8e})} against the standard single-stream baseline (\texttt{R8a}), DANG-Med achieves a \textbf{$+13.91$~pp ($+20.58\%$ relative) mDice}, \textbf{$+12.66$~pp ($+20.54\%$ relative) mIoU}, \textbf{$+10.66$~pp ($+21.08\%$ relative) BF1}, and \textbf{$-27.50\%$ HD95 reduction} on \textbf{ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($n=196$)}, while boosting \textbf{Adverse Endoscopy ($S_3$)} from $58.49\%$ to \textbf{$73.87\%$ mDice ($+15.39$~pp / $+26.31\%$ relative)}. Notably, on the hardest \texttt{Adverse\_Combined\_Severe} subset (simultaneous NBI shift + low-light vignette + defocus blur), unconfounded zero-shot \texttt{R9e} improves over \texttt{R9a} from $25.65\%$ to \textbf{$44.28\%$ mDice ($+72.66\%$ relative)}, and adverse-trained \texttt{R8e} reaches \textbf{$63.48\%$ mDice}.
  \item \textbf{In-Domain Preservation and Runtime Efficiency:} On in-domain \textbf{Kvasir-Test ($n=90$)}, \texttt{R9e} achieves \textbf{$93.06\%$ mDice}, \textbf{$88.55\%$ mIoU}, and \textbf{$72.46\%$ Boundary F1} (exceeding \texttt{R9a}'s $70.74\%$ BF1), confirming zero in-domain degradation. Thanks to \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM}, \texttt{R9e} runs at \textbf{$17.43$~FPS} ($43.32\text{M}$ trainable parameters out of $129.27\text{M}$ total, with $85.95\text{M}$ frozen in DINOv2) and completes both Track~$S_1$ and Track~Full evaluation across all $12$ cohorts in $42$~minutes on a single Kaggle T4 GPU.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented \textbf{DANG-Med}, a dual-stream attention-augmented and state-space aligned architecture for multi-center medical polyp segmentation. By uniting a 4-Scale Learned DPT Reassembler, a Supervised Dual-Prototype and Self-Support Margin Head, the Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE), a Zero-Shot Homomorphic Retinex Chromatic Stem with DINOv2-Conditioned Style Calibration, and an Uncertainty-Guided Axial Strip-SSM Decoder under a strictly unconfounded protocol, DANG-Med achieves $>15\%\text{--}43\%$ relative improvements across mDice, mIoU, Boundary F1, and HD95 on challenging few-shot, tiny/camouflaged, external OOD, and adverse endoscopic benchmarks.
