\begin{abstract}
Polyp segmentation networks that reach $\sim 93\%$ mean Dice (mDice) in domain still fail where it matters clinically: when only a few frames are labelled, on tiny or flat low-contrast lesions, and under acquisition shifts such as narrow-band imaging (NBI), low light, glare and blur. We present \textbf{DANG}, a dual-stream network that pairs a trainable ConvNeXt-Tiny with a frozen DINOv2-Base and uses the foundation features through three components: a dual-prototype margin head on multi-depth DINOv2 tokens, a prototype-anchored spatial support envelope (PASSE) that suppresses far-field false positives, and an uncertainty-guided directional strip decoder. We train on Kvasir-SEG only and test on deduplicated external cohorts and two diagnostic stress slices over five paired seeds, retraining PraNet, Polyp-PVT and EMCAD under the same protocol. With $40$ labelled images DANG reaches $51.0\%$ mDice on tiny/camouflaged lesions and $55.8\%$ on synthetic adverse endoscopy, ahead of every retrained model by $7$--$27$~pp, and its Boundary F1 exceeds theirs by $5$--$34$~pp. A control that adds the same frozen DINOv2 by plain concatenation gains nothing in this regime, so the improvement comes from how the foundation features are used, not from the extra parameters. With $800$ labelled images DANG beats PraNet but is statistically tied with Polyp-PVT and EMCAD. On the standard PraNet training split it matches a faithful Polyp-PVT reproduction on four test sets and exceeds it on ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($84.4\%$ vs.\ $78.9\%$). These gains cost efficiency: DANG has $130$M parameters ($43$M trainable) and runs at $17$~FPS on a T4, against $25$--$31$M parameters and $37$--$42$~FPS for the retrained models.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Colorectal cancer remains a leading cause of cancer death, and detecting and resecting polyps during colonoscopy reduces its incidence~\citep{jha2020kvasir,ali2023polypgen}. Pixel-level polyp segmentation supports this by delineating lesion margins. Its benchmarks, however, are close to saturation in domain: a ConvNeXt-Tiny~\citep{liu2022convnet} with a Feature Pyramid Network~\citep{lin2017fpn} already reaches about $93\%$ mDice on Kvasir-SEG~\citep{jha2020kvasir}, and architectural changes move this number by less than one point. In-domain scores therefore say little about whether a model is more robust.

Deployment stresses a model along three axes that in-domain benchmarks do not exercise:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Few labels ($S_1$).} Pixel-accurate contours require expert time, so a new centre may label only tens of frames. Trained on $40$ images, CNNs overfit to source textures and their boundaries degrade on external centres.
  \item \textbf{Tiny and camouflaged lesions ($S_2$).} Flat or diminutive polyps covering under $1.5\%$ of the frame, or with low contrast to the surrounding mucosa, are fragmented or missed by stride-$16/32$ features, especially on ETIS-LaribPolypDB~\citep{silva2014toward} and CVC-ColonDB~\citep{tajbakhsh2015automated}.
  \item \textbf{Acquisition shift ($S_3$).} Procedures switch between white-light and NBI-like spectra and suffer illumination fall-off, sensor noise, specular glare and motion blur.
\end{enumerate}

Vision foundation models such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2} and SAM~\citep{kirillov2023segment} provide features that transfer across domains, but their $14$-pixel patch grid is too coarse for lesion boundaries. Concatenating such features with a CNN is the obvious remedy; we show that it does not help when labels are scarce.

We present \textbf{DANG} (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}) and make four contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A deduplicated multi-centre stress benchmark.} Perceptual-hash and MD5 deduplication across Kvasir-SEG, six-centre PolypGen~\citep{ali2023polypgen} and the four PraNet external sets~\citep{fan2020pranet}, with a $40$-image few-shot track and two stress slices: natural tiny/camouflaged lesions and synthetic adverse acquisitions (Figure~\ref{fig:examples}).
  \item \textbf{Prototype-anchored use of foundation features.} Multi-depth DINOv2 tokens feed a dual-prototype margin head with test-time self-support, and the \textbf{prototype-anchored spatial support envelope (PASSE)} turns this margin into a mask that suppresses false positives far from the lesion and reinforces low-contrast lesion cores.
  \item \textbf{An uncertainty-guided strip decoder.} Fused horizontal, vertical and dilated depthwise strips, gated by the Bernoulli boundary variance $4p(1-p)$, refine boundaries where the coarser prediction is ambiguous.
  \item \textbf{A controlled five-seed evaluation against retrained state of the art.} PraNet~\citep{fan2020pranet}, Polyp-PVT~\citep{dong2021polyp} and EMCAD~\citep{rahman2024emcad} are retrained under our protocol, a concatenation control isolates the effect of the foundation features, and a standard-split experiment ties our protocol to published numbers. We report where DANG does not win, including the full-data regime and inference cost, and that its Retinex stem does not contribute.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Polyp segmentation.} Architectures progressed from U-Net~\citep{ronneberger2015unet} and UNet++~\citep{zhou2018unetplusplus} to reverse-attention networks: PraNet~\citep{fan2020pranet} mines boundary cues with parallel partial decoders and reverse attention, and CaraNet~\citep{lou2022caranet} adds axial context for small objects. Transformer encoders improved multi-centre generalisation in Polyp-PVT~\citep{dong2021polyp} and TransFuse~\citep{zhang2021transfuse}, and EMCAD~\citep{rahman2024emcad} pairs a PVTv2 encoder with an efficient multi-scale convolutional attention decoder.

\paragraph{Foundation models for polyps.} SAM-Mamba~\citep{dutta2024sammamba} and SAM-MaGuP~\citep{dutta2025sammagup} adapt SAM with Mamba-based priors and report strong zero-shot generalisation, at about $103$M parameters and $423$~GFLOPs~\citep{agnihotri2026litebound}. A second line distils foundation models into small networks: Polyp-DiFoM~\citep{agnihotri2025difom} distils SAM and DINOv2 into lightweight baselines, LiteBounD~\citep{agnihotri2026litebound} adds boundary-guided frequency-aware distillation, and Lite-$\pi$~\citep{agnihotri2026litepi} aligns foundation-model-specific prototypes with a lightweight network for real-time inference. DANG keeps the foundation model at inference and studies how to use its features when labels are scarce; it is complementary to distillation, which could compress it.

\paragraph{Adapting foundation models and directional operators.} Parameter-efficient fine-tuning such as LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} and dense-prediction heads such as DPT~\citep{ranftl2021vision} adapt frozen ViTs to segmentation. State-space models (Mamba~\citep{gu2023mamba}, VMamba~\citep{liu2024vmamba}, U-Mamba~\citep{ma2024umamba}) capture long-range context with directional scans; our strip decoder approximates directional context with fused depthwise strip convolutions instead.

\section{Method}
DANG maps an RGB frame $I\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times352\times352}$ to a polyp probability map $\hat Y=\sigma(Z)$ (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).

\subsection{Two streams}
\label{sec:stem}
The local stream is ConvNeXt-Tiny, producing features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$ at strides $s\in\{4,8,16,32\}$. The semantic stream is DINOv2-Base, frozen, with rank-$4$ convolutional LoRA adapters on its last four blocks that route low- and high-frequency token components to experts with dilations $1$--$3$. A Retinex chromatic stem~\citep{land1971lightness} removes specular highlights and divides out a smooth illumination estimate before both streams, and the global DINOv2 vector conditions a feature-space style calibration (with MixStyle~\citep{zhou2021mixstyle}) at strides $4$ and $8$. The stem is trained without synthetic corruptions; Section~\ref{sec:ablation} shows that it does not contribute.

\subsection{Multi-scale reassembly and dual-prototype head}
We take DINOv2 tokens from blocks $\{2,5,8,11\}$ on a $25\times25$ grid and project each to $D=192$ channels, $S_\ell$. A learned depth-mixing matrix $W=\mathrm{Softmax}(\Theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times4}$ produces one semantic map per decoder scale,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde S_s=\sum_{\ell} W_{s,\ell}S_\ell,\qquad \hat S_s=\phi_s\bigl(\mathrm{Up}_s(\tilde S_s,F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)})\bigr),
  \label{eq:dpt}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Up}_s$ at strides $8$ and $16$ predicts $3\times3$ reassembly kernels from the CNN features, so that upsampled semantic features snap to CNN edges.

The prototype head projects the reassembled tokens to $L_2$-normalised embeddings $\hat Z_{u,v}\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$ and compares them with $K=8$ foreground prototypes $p_k^+$ and $K=8$ background prototypes $p_k^-$, plus foreground and background anchors $a^\pm$ that track ground-truth token means during training:
\begin{align}
  \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) &= \tfrac{\tau}{\beta}\Bigl(\log\textstyle\sum_k e^{\beta\langle\hat Z_{u,v},p_k^+\rangle}\nonumber\\
  &\quad -\log\textstyle\sum_k e^{\beta\langle\hat Z_{u,v},p_k^-\rangle}\Bigr)\nonumber\\
  &\quad + w_{\text{a}}\bigl(\langle\hat Z_{u,v},a^+\rangle-\langle\hat Z_{u,v},a^-\rangle\bigr)+b.
  \label{eq:proto_prior}
\end{align}
At test time no mask is available, so the head also computes image-specific self-support centroids $c^+=\mathrm{Norm}(\sum p_{u,v}^2\hat Z_{u,v})$ and $c^-=\mathrm{Norm}(\sum(1-p_{u,v})^2\hat Z_{u,v})$ with $p=\sigma(\pi_{\text{prior}})$, giving the margin map
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\text{proto}}=\pi_{\text{prior}}+w_{\text{ss}}\bigl(\langle\hat Z,c^+\rangle-\langle\hat Z,c^-\rangle\bigr).
  \label{eq:proto_total}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prototype-anchored spatial support envelope (PASSE)}
CNN texture far from the lesion can cross the decision threshold and produce isolated false positives. PASSE builds a coarse semantic probability $P_{\text{sem}}=\sigma(0.5\,M_4^{\uparrow25}+0.5\,\pi_{\text{proto}})$ from the coarsest decoder logit $M_4$ and the prototype margin, dilates it into a smooth envelope
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}=\mathrm{AvgPool}_{3\times3}\bigl(\mathrm{MaxPool}_{5\times5}(P_{\text{sem}})\bigr),
  \label{eq:passe_env}
\end{equation}
and uses it to modulate the stride-$4$ decoder logit $Z_{88}$:
\begin{align}
  Z_{88}^{\text{P}} &= Z_{88}-\lambda_{\text{out}}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(t_{\text{out}}-\mathcal{E}^{\uparrow88}\bigr)\nonumber\\
  &\quad +\lambda_{\text{in}}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(P_{\text{sem}}^{\uparrow88}-t_{\text{in}}\bigr),\nonumber\\
  Z &= 0.8\,\bigl(Z_{88}^{\text{P}}\bigr)^{\uparrow352}+0.2\,\pi_{\text{proto}}^{\uparrow352}.
  \label{eq:passe_mod}
\end{align}
The first term suppresses logits outside the envelope and the second reinforces confident lesion cores ($t_{\text{out}}=0.22$, $\lambda_{\text{out}}=10$, $t_{\text{in}}=0.38$, $\lambda_{\text{in}}=1.2$).

\subsection{Strip fusion and uncertainty-guided decoder}
At strides $8$--$32$ the two streams are fused with a gated directional strip operator,
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Strip}(U,C) &= U+g_h\odot\mathcal{K}_{7\times1}\mathcal{K}_{1\times11}(U)\nonumber\\
  &\quad +g_v\odot\mathcal{K}_{1\times7}\mathcal{K}_{11\times1}(U)+g_d\odot\mathcal{K}_{3\times3,d=3}(U),
  \label{eq:stripssm}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{K}$ are depthwise convolutions and the gates $(g_h,g_v,g_d)=\sigma(\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}([U,C]))$ are conditioned on the other stream. Each top-down decoder stage receives the upsampled logit of the coarser stage, converts it to a foreground probability $A$, and computes the boundary uncertainty $U=4A(1-A)$, which peaks at $A=0.5$, and an outer ring $\mathrm{MaxPool}_{5\times5}(A)-A$. Both maps gate the strip operator and a Sobel edge refinement, so computation concentrates on ambiguous borders.

\subsection{Training objective}
Every model in this paper, including the baselines, is trained with the same loss:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L} &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{w}(Z,Y)+0.25\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{w}(Z,Y)\nonumber\\
  &\quad +0.1\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z,Y)+\tfrac{0.25}{3}\textstyle\sum_{j=2}^{4}\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{w}(M_j,Y)\nonumber\\
  &\quad +0.35\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}(\pi_{\text{proto}},Y_{25}).
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{align}
The per-image weights $w$ grow as lesion area shrinks, the Tversky trade-off shifts towards recall for small lesions, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}$ penalises predicted probability outside dilations of the ground truth, and $M_j$ are deep-supervision logits. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}$ applies the same Dice, cross-entropy and Tversky terms to the prototype margin and is present only in models with the prototype head. The published baselines are trained with their official losses; one Polyp-PVT run uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:loss} as a loss-matched control.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Data and cohorts}
We deduplicate every evaluation set against the training data with MD5 hashes and a $64$-bit perceptual difference hash ($\text{dHash}\le4$), and remove Kvasir frames that public composites redistribute under other names.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Kvasir-SEG}~\citep{jha2020kvasir}: $800$ training images (\emph{Full} track), a fixed $40$-image subset of them ($5\%$, the few-shot track $S_1$), $95$ validation and $90$ in-domain test images.
  \item \textbf{PolypGen}~\citep{ali2023polypgen}: six centres; a development set ($n=258$) and an extended holdout ($n=974$) that was not inspected during design and serves as a confirmation cohort.
  \item \textbf{PraNet external sets}~\citep{fan2020pranet}: CVC-300 ($60$), CVC-ClinicDB ($61$), CVC-ColonDB ($379$) and ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($196$), $696$ images after deduplication. ClinicDB is unseen because we train on Kvasir only.
  \item \textbf{$S_2$, tiny and camouflaged} ($n=310$): natural lesions from PolypGen-Dev, ETIS and ColonDB covering under $1.5\%$ of the frame, or under $3\%$ with low boundary contrast.
  \item \textbf{$S_3$, adverse acquisition} ($n=258$): PolypGen-Dev frames under one of four deterministic corruptions: NBI-like spectral shift, low light with vignetting and shot noise, specular glare with blur, and a combined severe mode.
\end{itemize}
$S_2$ and $S_3$ are diagnostic slices of the external cohorts, not independent patient cohorts, and $S_3$ is synthetic.

\subsection{Training and evaluation}
All models are trained at $352\times352$ with batch size $16$, AdamW, warm-up followed by cosine decay, an exponential moving average of the weights, and mixed precision. The few-shot track trains for $40$ epochs and the Full track for $20$. The checkpoint is selected by Kvasir validation mDice only. Every model, including the published baselines, uses the same data, augmentation, schedule, seeds and evaluation code; for a given seed all models see the same $40$-image subset.

Predictions are thresholded at $0.5$. We report mDice, Boundary F1 (BF1, $2$-pixel tolerance) and the 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95, pixels at $352\times352$). Differences between models are tested with paired $t$-tests over seeds $42$--$46$, and each family of $12$ comparisons is corrected with Holm's procedure. Inference speed is measured at batch size $1$ in FP16 on an NVIDIA T4.

\subsection{Standard split and published numbers}
\label{sec:std}
Our protocol trains on Kvasir only, so its numbers are not comparable to the literature, which trains on $900$ Kvasir and $550$ ClinicDB images. To tie the two, we trained DANG and Polyp-PVT on this standard split for $50$ epochs, kept the final weight average without test-set checkpoint selection, and evaluated at native resolution (three seeds; Table~\ref{tab:std}). Our Polyp-PVT reproduction is within $1.1$~pp of its published scores on every set, which validates the pipeline. DANG matches it on Kvasir, ClinicDB, ColonDB and CVC-300 (all differences within $\pm1.1$~pp, not significant) and exceeds it on ETIS-LaribPolypDB by $5.5$~pp mDice ($p=0.026$) and $11.1$~pp BF1 ($p=0.007$; paired, $n=3$, uncorrected). ETIS, the set with the smallest and least textured lesions, is where the few-shot results predict an advantage.

Table~\ref{tab:multiseed} and Figures~\ref{fig:multiseed}--\ref{fig:deltas} isolate the components.

\paragraph{Foundation features alone do not help.} The concatenation control (S) has the same frozen DINOv2, the same adapters and nearly the same size as DANG ($120$M parameters), yet in the few-shot regime it is indistinguishable from the 31M-parameter baseline ($44.9\%$ vs.\ $44.7\%$ on $S_1\times S_2$, $46.3\%$ vs.\ $45.6\%$ on $S_1\times S_3$). DANG, with $10$M more parameters, gains $6.1$~pp ($p=0.003$) and $9.5$~pp ($p<0.001$) over the control on these cohorts and $14.1$~pp BF1 on tiny/camouflaged lesions. Parameter count therefore does not explain DANG's advantage; the prototype, PASSE and strip-decoder path does.

\paragraph{Gains over the baseline.} DANG raises few-shot BF1 by $11.9$--$14.4$~pp on all three cohorts and mDice on adverse acquisitions by $10.2$~pp, all significant after correction. With full data it gains $6.3$~pp on ETIS and $3.2$~pp on the PraNet external sets while in-domain accuracy is unchanged ($92.95\%$ vs.\ $92.92\%$ on Kvasir test). On the untouched PolypGen extended holdout the gain is smaller but significant ($+1.4$~pp, $p=0.009$; $+1.2$~pp on the subset that shares no video sequence with the development set, $p=0.004$). Full-data $S_3$ mDice rises from $55.8\%$ to $67.4\%$, most on low-light ($+18.7$~pp) and combined-severe frames ($+23.0$~pp).

\paragraph{The stem is a negative result.} Removing the Retinex stem and style calibration (C) keeps almost all of the improvement, and no mDice difference between C and F survives correction. The only uncorrected mDice effect of the stem is a loss of $4.3$~pp on NBI frames ($p=0.008$). One plausible reason, which we did not test, is that the stem standardises colour towards white-light Kvasir statistics and so maps the green-dominant NBI palette onto a distribution the prototype head has not seen. We keep the full model as DANG for consistency with the main experiments, but recommend the stem-free variant for deployment.

\paragraph{HD95.} An empty prediction receives a fixed HD95 penalty of $124.5$~px, which is smaller than the HD95 of $10.9\%$ of non-empty predictions, so an empty mask can score better than a mask with a distant false positive. Empty-prediction rates are similar for DANG and the baseline ($28.5\%$ vs.\ $27.8\%$ on $S_1\times S_2$). With larger penalties the direction of the HD95 differences holds but their size shrinks, and on two cohorts they are not significant even with the default penalty. We therefore base our claims on mDice and BF1.

\subsection{Efficiency}
DANG is the most expensive model in Table~\ref{tab:sota}: $129.9$M parameters, of which $86.6$M are frozen DINOv2 weights and $43.3$M are trained, and $17.4$~FPS against $36.5$--$42.4$~FPS for the retrained models and $91.9$~FPS for the ConvNeXt baseline. The concatenation control, which runs the same two encoders, reaches $38.4$~FPS, so most of DANG's extra latency lies in the decoding path (prototype head, PASSE, strip fusion and decoder) rather than in the foundation model. Removing the stem raises throughput to $19.3$~FPS. At $17$~FPS DANG suits second-reader and offline review; real-time use at $25$--$30$~Hz needs a faster GPU, an optimised implementation of the decoding path, or distillation into a smaller network~\citep{agnihotri2025difom,agnihotri2026litepi}. For reference, SAM-based polyp models are larger still (about $103$M parameters and $423$~GFLOPs~\citep{agnihotri2026litebound}).

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Training length of the retrained baselines.} In the few-shot track, the retrained models reached their best validation score in the last $5$ of $40$ epochs, so they may not have converged; DANG peaked between epochs $25$ and $39$. Longer training could narrow the few-shot gap.
  \item \textbf{Fixed few-shot subset and single source centre.} The $40$-image subset is the same for all seeds, so the variance in Table~\ref{tab:sota} reflects optimisation, not the choice of images. All main models are trained on Kvasir only.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive design.} PASSE thresholds and loss radii were tuned while inspecting PolypGen-Dev, $S_2$, $S_3$ and ETIS, so these cohorts are not fully held out. The extended PolypGen holdout was not used for design, and the gains there are smaller.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic and derived cohorts.} $S_3$ is synthetic, not paired physical NBI/white-light acquisitions, and $S_2$ and $S_3$ are slices of the external cohorts.
  \item \textbf{Statistical power.} Five seeds give four degrees of freedom and the standard-split experiment three seeds; several raw $p<0.05$ effects do not survive correction.
  \item \textbf{Confounded prototype loss.} Models with the prototype head also receive $\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}$, so their gains reflect both, and we have not run a full factorial over PASSE, the prototype loss and the strip decoder.
  \item \textbf{Cost.} DANG is $2$--$2.5\times$ slower than the retrained models and has $4$--$5\times$ more parameters.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Ethics and data.} No new patient data were collected. All experiments use public, de-identified datasets (Kvasir-SEG~\citep{jha2020kvasir}, PolypGen~\citep{ali2023polypgen}, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-ColonDB~\citep{tajbakhsh2015automated}, CVC-300 and ETIS-LaribPolypDB~\citep{silva2014toward}) under their research licences.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and AI assistance.} Code, deduplication manifests, per-image evaluation logs and the scripts that regenerate every table and figure from the raw logs will be released. AI agents assisted with experiment orchestration, log aggregation, figure generation and drafting (Google Gemini; Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic), and independent agents (Codex, OpenAI; Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic) audited the aggregations, code-to-equation consistency and statistics. The authors take responsibility for all content.

\section{Conclusion}
DANG uses a frozen DINOv2 through a prototype margin head, a prototype-anchored support envelope and an uncertainty-guided strip decoder. When labels are scarce it outperforms PraNet, Polyp-PVT and EMCAD retrained under the same protocol, by up to $16.6$~pp mDice and $20.8$~pp BF1 over the strongest of them, and on the standard split it matches Polyp-PVT on four test sets and exceeds it on ETIS. A same-size concatenation control shows that the gain comes from how the foundation features are used, not from their parameters. With abundant labels DANG ties the best retrained models, and it is $2$--$2.5\times$ slower. Next steps are longer few-shot training of the baselines, resampled few-shot subsets, a lighter backbone and an optimised decoding path, and evaluation on physical NBI acquisitions.
