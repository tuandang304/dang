\begin{abstract}
Polyp segmentation networks that reach $\sim 93\%$ mean Dice (mDice) on in-domain Kvasir-SEG still fail under the conditions that matter clinically: few labelled frames from a new centre, tiny or flat low-contrast lesions, and acquisition shifts such as narrow-band imaging (NBI), low light, glare and blur. We present \textbf{DANG-Med}, a dual-stream network that couples a trainable ConvNeXt-Tiny local stream with a frozen DINOv2-Base semantic stream adapted by frequency-routed mixture-of-experts convolutional LoRA. On top of the two streams, DANG-Med adds (i) a 4-scale learned DPT reassembler feeding a dual-prototype, image-adaptive self-support margin head; (ii) a prototype-anchored spatial support envelope (PASSE) that suppresses far-field false positives; (iii) an uncertainty-guided directional strip decoder gated by the Bernoulli boundary variance $4p(1-p)$; and (iv) a Retinex chromatic stem with DINO-conditioned style calibration, trained without synthetic corruptions. All models are trained on Kvasir-SEG only and evaluated on deduplicated external cohorts (six PolypGen centres, CVC-300, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-ColonDB, ETIS-LaribPolypDB) and two diagnostic stress slices, against a ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN baseline and a dual-stream concatenation control, over five paired seeds. With $40$ labelled images, DANG-Med raises mDice from $44.7\%$ to $51.0\%$ on tiny/camouflaged lesions ($+6.3$~pp, $p=0.014$) and from $45.6\%$ to $55.8\%$ on synthetic adverse endoscopy ($+10.2$~pp, $p=0.005$), and lifts Boundary F1 by $12$--$14$~pp on every few-shot cohort. With $800$ labelled images it reaches $78.9\%$ mDice on ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($+6.3$~pp, $p=0.005$) and $81.8\%$ on the four PraNet external sets ($+3.2$~pp, $p=0.003$), while matching the baseline in domain. The concatenation control gains nothing in the few-shot regime, and a five-seed ablation attributes the improvement to the prototype, PASSE and strip-decoder path: removing the Retinex stem and style calibration changes no mDice cohort significantly and recovers $4.3$~pp on NBI-shifted frames. HD95 improvements are significant on only some cohorts and depend on the empty-prediction penalty, and the adverse-endoscopy cohort is synthetic; we report both limits with the results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and early endoscopic detection and resection of adenomatous and sessile serrated polyps during optical colonoscopy significantly reduces CRC incidence~\citep{jha2020kvasir,ali2023polypgen}. Automated pixel-level polyp segmentation provides computer-aided delineation (CADe/CADx) of lesion margins during withdrawal. However, a substantial gap persists between in-domain benchmark saturation and multi-center clinical deployment. On standard in-domain white-light colonoscopy splits such as Kvasir-SEG~\citep{jha2020kvasir}, modern hierarchical convolutional networks (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny~\citep{liu2022convnet} with Feature Pyramid Networks~\citep{lin2017fpn}) already achieve $92.5\%\text{--}93.1\%$ mean Dice (mDice). Under such saturated in-domain conditions, architectural modifications yield modest differences ($<0.5\text{--}1.0$~percentage points), obscuring whether a proposed model genuinely improves out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness.

In clinical practice, endoscopic segmentation systems degrade across three compounding stress axes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Label-Scarce Multi-Center Adaptation ($S_1$):} Annotating pixel-accurate polyp contours requires expert gastroenterologists. When training under a strict $5\%\text{--}10\%$ annotation budget ($40\text{--}80$ labeled images), standard CNNs overfit to source-hospital background textures and suffer severe boundary degradation on external centers ($\text{Boundary F1} < 20\%$, $\text{HD95} > 70\,\text{px}$).
  \item \textbf{Natural Tiny and Low-Contrast Camouflaged Lesions ($S_2$):} Flat Paris~0-IIa/IIb adenomas and diminutive polyps occupying $<1.5\%$ of the endoscopic field of view (FOV) or exhibiting low RGB contrast ($<0.085$) against surrounding vascular mucosa are frequently missed or fragmented by stride-$16/32$ feature pooling, particularly on external cohorts such as ETIS-LaribPolypDB~\citep{silva2014toward} and CVC-ColonDB~\citep{tajbakhsh2015automated}.
  \item \textbf{Adverse Endoscopic Acquisition Shifts ($S_3$):} Endoscopic procedures frequently switch between White-Light Imaging (WLI) and Narrow-Band Imaging (NBI)-like spectral shifts, encounter distal-lumen illumination falloff ($\gamma > 2.2$ with sensor noise), and suffer from wet mucosal specular highlights coupled with motion/defocus blur.
\end{enumerate}

While Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2} and SAM~\citep{kirillov2023segment} offer strong semantic invariance across domains, their coarse patch grids ($14\times 14$ stride) lack fine sub-pixel boundary localization. Conversely, naive feature concatenation between a CNN encoder and a ViT backbone can degrade high-resolution CNN edge features when ViT patch tokens are bilinearly upsampled across lesion boundaries, and sequential 2D state-space scans (SS2D~\citep{liu2024vmamba}) incur substantial Python/CUDA kernel launch overhead on multi-scale feature pyramids.

In this work, we present \textbf{DANG-Med}, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}. Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{An auditable multi-centre stress evaluation.} An automated perceptual-hash and MD5 deduplication pipeline (\texttt{HYGIENE\_MANIFEST\_JSON}) across Kvasir-SEG, $6$-centre PolypGen~\citep{ali2023polypgen} (Dev $n=258$ and an extended holdout $n=974$ in Round~9/10; $n=272$ and $n=967$ in Round~8), and the four PraNet external benchmarks~\citep{fan2020pranet} ($n=696$ after removing $2$ near-duplicates of Kvasir), plus derived stress slices: $S_1$ few-shot training ($5\%$/$10\%$ of Kvasir), $S_2$ natural tiny/camouflaged lesions ($n=310/315$) and $S_3$ synthetic adverse acquisitions ($n=258/272$) (Figure~\ref{fig:examples}).
  \item \textbf{Prototype-anchored support for the decoder.} Multi-depth DINOv2 tokens are reassembled at four scales and projected onto an $L_2$-normalised prototype hypersphere that combines orthogonally initialised $K$-prototype cosine margins, training-only ground-truth EMA anchors, and test-time self-support centroids. The \textbf{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)} converts this margin into a dilated envelope that suppresses far-field false-positive specks and reinforces low-contrast lesion cores.
  \item \textbf{A directional strip decoder guided by boundary uncertainty.} \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM} replaces sequential state-space scans with fused horizontal ($1\times11$, $7\times1$), vertical ($11\times1$, $1\times7$) and dilated ($3\times3$, $d=3$) depthwise strips, modulated by the normalised Bernoulli variance $u=4p(1-p)$ and an outer morphological ring.
  \item \textbf{A five-seed paired evaluation that separates what helps from what does not.} Across seeds $42$--$46$ we compare the full model with a single-stream baseline, a dual-stream concatenation control and a no-stem ablation, and report Holm-adjusted tests, empty-prediction rates and HD95 penalty sensitivity. The ablation shows that the Retinex stem with DINO-conditioned style calibration~(\texttt{RetinexChromaticStem}, Section~\ref{sec:stem}), trained without synthetic corruptions, does not contribute and hurts NBI-shifted frames; we report it as a negative result.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Polyp Segmentation and Boundary-Aware Decoders}
Deep polyp segmentation architectures have progressed from U-Net~\citep{ronneberger2015unet} and UNet++~\citep{zhou2018unetplusplus} to reverse-attention and boundary-aware networks. PraNet~\citep{fan2020pranet} introduced parallel partial decoders and iterative reverse attention to mine boundary cues from Res2Net backbones. CaraNet~\citep{lou2022caranet} extended reverse attention with axial context and channel-wise feature pyramid modules for small medical objects. Subsequent Transformer-based architectures such as Polyp-PVT~\citep{dong2021polyp} and TransFuse~\citep{zhang2021transfuse} paired or replaced CNN encoders with Pyramid Vision Transformers to improve multi-center generalization on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB. Unlike dual-branch models that fuse features only at coarse $1/16$ and $1/32$ scales with unconstrained bilinear upsampling, DANG-Med performs 4-scale learned DPT depth mixing with content-aware edge-snapped upsampling and explicit spatial support gating at stride~$4$ ($88\times 88$).

\subsection{Vision Foundation Models and Parameter-Efficient Adaptation}
Self-supervised Vision Transformers pretrained via discriminative self-distillation, notably DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2}, exhibit strong part-level semantic consistency across domains, while promptable foundation models such as SAM~\citep{kirillov2023segment} and its medical adaptations require either bounding-box prompts or heavy decoder fine-tuning. Adapting VFMs to autonomous dense medical segmentation while preventing overfitting on small clinical datasets ($N \le 800$) benefits from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) such as LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} and Dense Prediction Transformers (DPT)~\citep{ranftl2021vision}. We freeze $100\%$ of the DINOv2-Base backbone ($86.58\text{M}$ frozen parameters logged across all dual-stream runs) and inject lightweight Frequency-Routed Mixture-of-Experts Convolutional LoRA (\textbf{Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA}) adapters into blocks $8\text{--}11$, decomposing spatial tokens into low-frequency context and high-frequency edge energy routed across dilation rates $d \in \{1,2,3\}$.

\subsection{State-Space and Directional Strip Operators in Medical Vision}
Structured State-Space Sequence Models (S4, Mamba~\citep{gu2023mamba}) and 2D visual extensions (VMamba~\citep{liu2024vmamba}, U-Mamba~\citep{ma2024umamba}) model long-range spatial dependencies. However, executing recursive 4-directional 1D scans in Python across multi-scale 2D feature pyramids incurs substantial kernel launch overhead. Motivated by the connection between truncated state-space convolutional kernels and directional strip convolutions, our \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM} captures horizontal, vertical, and dilated contextual dependencies using fused depthwise strip convolutions gated by cross-stream conditioning, achieving $17.4\text{--}20.0$~FPS (measured at batch size $1$, $352\times 352$, FP16 on NVIDIA T4).

\section{Methodology: The DANG-Med Architecture}
Given an input endoscopic RGB image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ ($H = W = 352$), DANG-Med (\texttt{src/dang/model.py}) predicts a dense foreground polyp probability map $\hat{Y} = \sigma(Z) \in [0, 1]^{B \times 1 \times H \times W}$. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the complete pipeline.

\subsection{Retinex Chromatic Stem and DINO-Conditioned Style Calibrator}
\label{sec:stem}
Endoscopic frames from external centers or adverse modes exhibit non-uniform illumination and spectral shifts. Let $X_{\text{rgb}} \in [0, 1]^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ denote the unnormalized RGB image entering \texttt{RetinexChromaticStem} (instantiated in code whenever \texttt{use\_style\_calib=True}).
\paragraph{1) Specular Highlight Inpainting:} Mucosal glare saturates all three RGB channels. We detect specular pixels via a smooth minimum-channel gate $w_{\text{glare}} = \sigma(18.0 \cdot (\min_c X_{\text{rgb}} - 0.82))$ and blend them with a $15\times 15$ local average filter $\bar{X}_{15}$:
\begin{equation}
  X_{\text{deg}} = (1 - w_{\text{glare}}) X_{\text{rgb}} + w_{\text{glare}} \bar{X}_{15}.
  \label{eq:deglare}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{2) Multi-Scale Homomorphic Retinex Division:} Following Retinex theory~\citep{land1971lightness}, which approximates image intensity as the product of illumination $L(x)$ and reflectance $R(x)$, we estimate illumination envelopes $L_{31} = \text{AvgPool}_{31\times 31}(X_{\text{deg}}) + \epsilon$ and $L_{15} = \text{AvgPool}_{15\times 15}(X_{\text{deg}}) + \epsilon$ ($\epsilon = 0.03$) and recover log-reflectance:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{R} = \log(X_{\text{deg}} + \epsilon) - \bigl(0.65 \log L_{31} + 0.35 \log L_{15}\bigr).
  \label{eq:retinex}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{3) Source EMA Standardization and Anomaly Gate:} During training on source Kvasir-SEG images, the stem updates exponential moving average reference buffers $(\mu_{\text{ref}}, \sigma_{\text{ref}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times 3\times 1\times 1}$ (in training mode only; frozen during \texttt{model.eval()}). In our Round~9/10 protocol (\texttt{R9d}, \texttt{R9e}, \texttt{R10d}, \texttt{R10e}), \texttt{aug\_prob} inside the stem is hard-set to $0.0$ so no synthetic adverse corruptions are generated during training (source-trained with mild photometric jitter $\gamma \in [0.8, 1.25]$, evaluated zero-shot on unseen $S_3$ synthetic corruptions where $\gamma = 2.55$). In Round~8 (\texttt{R8e}, commit \texttt{9539e96}), \texttt{aug\_prob=0.55} synthesizes NBI, low-light ($\gamma=2.35$), glare+blur, and combined corruptions with $0.55$ contrast compression during training; thus \texttt{R8e}'s $S_3$ score reflects in-family corruption-matched training rather than zero-shot OOD generalization. Combining instance-standardized reflectance $\hat{R}$ and global chromaticity $\hat{X}_{\text{deg}}$, the restored image is $X_{\text{rest}} = \text{clip}((0.55 \hat{X}_{\text{deg}} + 0.45 \hat{R}) \sigma_{\text{ref}} + \mu_{\text{ref}}, 0, 1)$. A photometric anomaly score $A_{\text{phot}}$ measures the normalized deviation of $( \mu(X_{\text{deg}}), \sigma(X_{\text{deg}}) )$ from $(\mu_{\text{ref}}, \sigma_{\text{ref}})$ plus glare fraction, gating a learned $3\times 3$ residual refinement branch (with minimum blend floor $0.04$).

At high-resolution ConvNeXt stages $s_4$ ($88\times 88$) and $s_8$ ($44\times 44$), \textbf{DINOFreqStyleCalibrator} first applies feature-space MixStyle~\citep{zhou2021mixstyle} with probability $p=0.5$ ($\text{Beta}(0.1,0.1)$ instance-stat mixing across the batch during training only) and then decomposes local CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}$ into a $9\times 9$ low-frequency component $F_{\text{low}}$ and high-frequency detail $F_{\text{high}} = F_{\text{loc}} - F_{\text{low}}$, modulating both via affine parameters $(\gamma_{\text{low}}, \beta_{\text{low}}, \gamma_{\text{high}})$ predicted from the global DINOv2 context vector $g_{\text{dino}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768}$.

\subsection{4-Scale DPT Reassembly and Dual-Prototype Self-Support Margin Head}
We extract spatial token grids $\{T_\ell\}_{\ell \in \{2,5,8,11\}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768 \times 25 \times 25}$ from DINOv2-Base (with Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA of rank $r=4$ on blocks $8\text{--}11$) and project each to $D = 192$ channels: $S_\ell = \text{ConvBNAct}_{1\times 1}(T_\ell)$.
\paragraph{4-Scale Learned DPT Reassembler:} We learn a $4\times 4$ depth-mixing matrix $W = \text{Softmax}(\Theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}$ across scales $s \in \{0,1,2,3\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{S}_s = \sum_{\ell=0}^{3} W_{s,\ell} S_\ell, \quad \hat{S}_s = \phi_s\bigl(\text{Up}_s(\tilde{S}_s, F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)})\bigr),
  \label{eq:dpt}
\end{equation}
where scales $s_8$ and $s_{16}$ use content-aware \textbf{Edge-Snapped Semantic Upsampling} (predicting $3\times 3$ local reassembly kernels from CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$, initialized to bilinear interpolation).

\paragraph{Dual-Prototype and Self-Support Margin Head:} We concatenate $[S_1, S_2, S_3]$, project to $D_p = 128$ dimensions, and $L_2$-normalize across channels to obtain unit token embeddings $\hat{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_p \times 25 \times 25}$. We initialize $K=8$ learnable foreground prototypes $\{p_k^+\}_{k=1}^K$ and $K=8$ background prototypes $\{p_k^-\}_{k=1}^K$ orthogonally, alongside training-gated ground-truth EMA support anchors $(a^+, a^-)$ updated only during training from downsampled mask occupancy ($M_{25} \ge 0.35$ for foreground, $M_{25} \le 0.10$ for background). The prior cosine margin logit at each $25\times 25$ patch $(u,v)$ is:
\begin{align}
  \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) &= \tfrac{\tau}{\beta} \Bigl( \log\sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, \hat{p}_k^+\rangle} - \log\sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, \hat{p}_k^-\rangle} \Bigr) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + 10\,w_{\text{ema}} \bigl(\langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, a^+\rangle - \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, a^-\rangle\bigr) + b.
  \label{eq:proto_prior}
\end{align}
During inference (without ground-truth masks), we compute image-specific self-support centroids $c^+ = \text{Norm}(\sum_{u,v} p_{u,v}^2 \hat{Z}_{u,v})$ and $c^- = \text{Norm}(\sum_{u,v} (1-p_{u,v})^2 \hat{Z}_{u,v})$ where $p_{u,v} = \sigma(\pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v))$, yielding the $25\times 25$ prototype margin map:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\text{proto}}(u,v) = \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) + 8w_{\text{ss}}\bigl(\langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, c^+\rangle - \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, c^-\rangle\bigr).
  \label{eq:proto_total}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)}
While prototype priors strengthen semantic localization on detected lesions, uncalibrated high-frequency CNN texture noise far from the true polyp can cross the $0.5$ threshold, inflating false positives and $\text{HD95}$. To suppress far-field false positives without eroding lesion interiors, \textbf{PASSE} constructs a $25\times 25$ semantic probability map $P_{\text{sem}} = \sigma(0.5 M_4^{\uparrow 25} + 0.5 \pi_{\text{proto}})$ from the coarsest decoder stage logit $M_4$ and prototype logit $\pi_{\text{proto}}$ (falling back to $P_{\text{sem}} = \sigma(M_4^{\uparrow 25})$ when the prototype head is disabled, as in variant \texttt{R9d}), and computes a dilated smooth support envelope:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_{\text{env}} = \text{AvgPool}_{3\times 3}\bigl(\text{MaxPool}_{5\times 5}(P_{\text{sem}})\bigr) \in [0,1]^{25\times 25}.
  \label{eq:passe_env}
\end{equation}
Upsampling $\mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}$ and $P_{\text{sem}}$ to stride~$4$ ($88\times 88$), PASSE modulates the stride-$4$ decoder logit $Z_{88}$ via two spatial terms, after which the final output logit blends the refined decoder prediction with the upsampled prototype margin when the prototype head is active ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{p}} \equiv \mathbb{I}[\text{proto}] = 1$):
\begin{align}
  Z_{88}^{\text{PASSE}} &= Z_{88} - \tfrac{2.2}{0.22}\,\text{ReLU}\bigl(0.22 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}^{\uparrow 88}\bigr) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + 1.2\,\text{ReLU}\bigl(P_{\text{sem}}^{\uparrow 88} - 0.38\bigr), \nonumber \\
  Z &= (1 - 0.2\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{p}})\bigl(Z_{88}^{\text{PASSE}}\bigr)^{\uparrow 352} + 0.2\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{p}}\,\pi_{\text{proto}}^{\uparrow 352}.
  \label{eq:passe_mod}
\end{align}

\subsection{Uncertainty-Guided Directional Strip Decoder}
At each pyramid scale $s \in \{s_8, s_{16}, s_{32}\}$, \textbf{GDSSAFusion} aligns local CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$ and semantic features $\hat{S}_s$ using \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM}, a gated finite-kernel directional strip operator:
\begin{align}
  \text{StripSSM}(U, C) &= U + g_h \odot \mathcal{K}_{7\times 1}(\mathcal{K}_{1\times 11}(U)) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + g_v \odot \mathcal{K}_{1\times 7}(\mathcal{K}_{11\times 1}(U)) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + g_d \odot \mathcal{K}_{3\times 3, d=3}(U),
  \label{eq:stripssm}
\end{align}
where $(g_h, g_v, g_d) = \sigma(\text{Conv}_{1\times 1}([U, C]))$ are input-conditioned directional gates and $\mathcal{K}$ denotes depthwise strip convolutions. In the top-down decoder ($P_4 \to P_3 \to P_2 \to P_1$), each \textbf{UncertaintyStripDecoderStage} computes from the coarser stage's logit $M_{s+1}^{\uparrow}$ three spatial priors: (i) foreground probability $A_{\text{fg}} = \sigma(M_{s+1}^{\uparrow})$, (ii) normalized Bernoulli boundary variance $U_s = 4 A_{\text{fg}}(1 - A_{\text{fg}}) \in [0,1]$ which peaks at $1.0$ along ambiguous borders ($A_{\text{fg}} = 0.5$), and (iii) an outer morphological ring halo $R_s = \text{clip}(\text{MaxPool}_{5\times 5}(A_{\text{fg}}) - A_{\text{fg}}, 0, 1)$, followed by A2BD Sobel-$L_1$ edge refinement.

\subsection{Training Objective and Variant Configuration Matrix}
In Round~9 and Round~10 (\texttt{R9a}--\texttt{R9e}, \texttt{R10a}--\texttt{R10e}), all arms use the exact same loss implementation \texttt{compute\_total\_loss} (\texttt{src/dang/train.py}, lines 103--140):
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{\tilde{w}}(Z, Y) + 0.25\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{\tilde{w},\alpha(a)}(Z, Y) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + 0.10\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z, Y) + \tfrac{0.25}{3}\sum_{j=2}^4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{\tilde{w}}(M_j, Y) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + 0.35\,\mathbb{I}_{\text{p}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}(\pi_{\text{proto}}, Y_{25}),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{align}
where $\mathbb{I}_{\text{p}} \equiv \mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]$ as in Eq.~(\ref{eq:passe_mod}), $a = 100 \cdot \bar{Y}$ is the ground-truth lesion area percentage, $w_i = \text{clip}((3.0 / (a_i + 0.35))^{0.40}, 1.0, 2.4)$ is normalized per batch via $\tilde{w}_i = w_i / \bar{w}$, $\alpha_{\text{fp}}(a) = 0.45 + 0.15\sigma((a - 1.5)/0.5)$ and $\beta_{\text{fn}}(a) = 1 - \alpha_{\text{fp}}(a)$ adapt Tversky supervision by lesion area, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z, Y)$ averages $\sigma(Z_x)(1 - \text{Dilate}_r(Y)_x)$ across four dilation radii $r \in \{4,8,14,20\}\,\text{px}$ to penalize far-field false positives across all five arms. The auxiliary prototype supervision term $0.35\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}$ (applying $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{\tilde{w}} + 0.25\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{\tilde{w},\alpha(a)}$ to $\pi_{\text{proto}}$ vs.\ $Y_{25}$) is active whenever the architecture includes the prototype head ($\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=1$ in \texttt{R9c}, \texttt{R9e}, \texttt{R8c}, \texttt{R8e}; $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$ in \texttt{R9a}, \texttt{R9b}, \texttt{R9d}, \texttt{R8a}, \texttt{R8b}, \texttt{R8d}). Specifically, the five Round~9 arms (differing only in \texttt{src/dang/config.py}) are configured as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9a} (Single-Stream Baseline, run \texttt{f328e771}, commit \texttt{e1379f6}):} ConvNeXt-Tiny + FPN decoder ($30.96\text{M}$ trainable / $30.96\text{M}$ total params), trained with Eq.~(\ref{eq:loss}) ($\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9b} (Dual-Stream SafeConcat Control, run \texttt{e770b25c}, commit \texttt{946256c}):} ConvNeXt-Tiny + frozen DINOv2-Base ($86.58\text{M}$) with Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA + \texttt{fusion\_mode=safe\_concat} + FPN decoder ($33.57\text{M}$ trainable / $120.15\text{M}$ total params, $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9c} (DANG-v4.1 ProtoDPT + PASSE + UncStrip, No Stem, run \texttt{f676224f}, commit \texttt{1218ebb}):} Enables \texttt{use\_proto\_dpt=true}, \texttt{use\_passe\_envelope=true}, \texttt{fusion\_mode=proto\_dpt\_strip\_ssa}, \texttt{decoder\_mode=uncertainty\_strip\_ssm}, \texttt{use\_style\_calib=false} ($42.80\text{M}$ trainable / $129.38\text{M}$ total params, $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=1$).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9d} (DANG-v4.1 RetinexStem + StyleCalib + PASSE + UncStrip, No ProtoDPT, run \texttt{5356b752}, commit \texttt{6d252bb}):} Enables \texttt{use\_style\_calib=true} (activating \texttt{RetinexChromaticStem} with zero-shot \texttt{aug\_prob=0.0} and MixStyle + \texttt{DINOFreqStyleCalibrator}), \texttt{use\_passe\_envelope=true}, \texttt{fusion\_mode=safe\_gd\_ssa\_v2}, \texttt{decoder\_mode=uncertainty\_strip\_ssm}, \texttt{use\_proto\_dpt=false} ($39.78\text{M}$ trainable / $126.36\text{M}$ total params, $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$).

\section{Experimental Setup and Data Hygiene}
\subsection{Datasets, Deduplication Hygiene, and Evaluation Cohorts}
Public composite polyp repositories (e.g., \texttt{combo\_polyp} / PolypGen) contain UUID-named Kvasir-SEG images in their raw splits and occasional near-duplicate frames. We enforce an automated deduplication pipeline (\texttt{src/dang/data.py}, logged via \texttt{HYGIENE\_MANIFEST\_JSON}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{In-Domain Kvasir-SEG ($n=1{,}000$ raw):} Shuffled (\texttt{seed=20260101}) into \textbf{Train-Full ($n=800$)}, \textbf{Train-$S_1$ Few-Shot ($5\%$, $n=40$ in R9/R10; $10\%$, $n=80$ in R8)}, \textbf{Val ($n=95$)}, and \textbf{ID-Test ($n=90$)}, where any validation or test image with $64$-bit perceptual difference hash distance $\text{dHash}_{64} \le 4$ to any training image is dropped ($5$ in Val, $10$ in ID-Test).
  \item \textbf{PolypGen 6-Center Out-of-Distribution (\texttt{C1}--\texttt{C6})~\citep{ali2023polypgen}:} Kvasir UUID filenames ($204$ in Dev, $796$ in Extended), empty masks ($30$ in Dev, $86$ in Extended), exact MD5 duplicates, and frames with $\min_{x \in \text{Kvasir}} \text{dHash}_{64} \le 4$ ($14$ in Dev, $78$ in Extended) are removed, yielding \textbf{PolypGen-Dev ($n=258$ in R9/R10; $n=272$ in R8 prior to the $\text{dHash}_{64}\le 4$ filter)} and \textbf{PolypGen-Extended Holdout ($n=974$ in R9/R10 after also filtering $47$ Dev near-duplicates and $49$ intra-Extended near-duplicates; $n=967$ in R8)}. We note that $74$ frames in PolypGen-Extended share \texttt{C4} video sequence prefixes with Dev; we therefore also track sequence-disjoint subsets in our logs.
  \item \textbf{PraNet Official External Benchmarks ($n=696$ total across 4 datasets)~\citep{fan2020pranet}:} After removing $2$ frames with exact/FOV-cropped $\text{dHash}_{64} \le 1$ overlap against Kvasir-SEG ($1$ in CVC-ClinicDB, $1$ in CVC-ColonDB), we evaluate: \textbf{CVC-300 ($n=60$)}, \textbf{CVC-ClinicDB ($n=61$)}, \textbf{CVC-ColonDB ($n=379$)}, and \textbf{ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($n=196$)}.
  \item \textbf{Derived Clinical Stress Track $S_2$ (Natural Tiny \& Camouflaged Polyps, $n=310$ in R9/R10; $n=315$ in R8):} Pooled across PolypGen-Dev, ETIS-LaribPolypDB, and CVC-ColonDB for natural lesions with area $<1.5\%$ of FOV (\texttt{S2\_Tiny\_LT1.5pct}, $n=188$) or area $<3.0\%$ with local boundary RGB contrast $<0.085$ (\texttt{S2\_Camouflaged\_LowContrast}, $n=122$). Because $S_2$ and $S_3$ are derived from the external cohorts above to isolate specific clinical failure modes, they are diagnostic stress slices rather than statistically independent patient cohorts.
  \item \textbf{Derived Clinical Stress Track $S_3$ (Synthetic Adverse Endoscopy, $n=258$ in R9/R10; $n=272$ in R8):} Deterministic transformations of PolypGen-Dev frames across $4$ regimes: \texttt{Adverse\_NBI\_Spectral}, \texttt{Adverse\_LowLight\_Lumen} ($\gamma=2.55$ + vignette + shot noise), \texttt{Adverse\_Specular\_Glare\_Blur}, and \texttt{Adverse\_Combined\_Severe}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implementation Details and Evaluation Metrics}
All models are trained at $352\times 352$ resolution ($350\times 350 = 25\times 25$ patches for DINOv2-Base) with batch size $16$, AdamW ($\text{lr}_{\text{cnn}} = 1.6\times 10^{-4}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{new}} = 4.0\times 10^{-4}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{peft}} = 1.0\times 10^{-3}$; weight decay $10^{-4}$ on CNN/head parameters and $0.0$ on PEFT/bias/normalization parameters), cosine annealing with warmup, EMA decay $0.992$, and FP16 mixed precision on NVIDIA T4 GPUs. Track~$S_1$ trains for $40$~epochs ($\sim 3.4\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9a}, $\sim 11.8\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9e}), and Track~Full trains for $20$~epochs ($\sim 20.4\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9a}, $\sim 72\text{--}80\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9e}). Checkpoint selection uses Kvasir-Val mDice exclusively (\texttt{model.eval()} never receives ground-truth masks).

At $352\times 352$ resolution with threshold $0.5$ (\texttt{src/dang/metrics.py}), we report \textbf{mDice ($\%$)}, \textbf{mIoU ($\%$)}, \textbf{Boundary F1 ($\%$)} ($1\text{-px}$ morphological contour with $2\text{-px}$ dilation tolerance), and \textbf{95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance ($\text{HD95}$, $\text{px}$)}. When a prediction mask is empty while the ground-truth mask is non-empty (or vice versa), $\text{BF1}=0$ and our implementation assigns a fixed finite quarter-diagonal penalty $\text{HD95}_{\text{empty}} = 0.25\sqrt{352^2+352^2} \approx 124.45\,\text{px}$; we also report non-empty-only Hausdorff distance (\texttt{hd95\_non\_empty}), empty-prediction miss rates (\texttt{ep}), and sensitivity across half-diagonal ($248.90\,\text{px}$) and full-diagonal ($497.80\,\text{px}$) penalties in Section~\ref{sec:hd95}.

\subsection{Main Result: Five Paired Seeds}
\label{sec:multiseed}
Table~\ref{tab:multiseed} and Figure~\ref{fig:multiseed} report the primary Round~9/10 comparison: four arms, five seeds each, identical data, subset and primary loss. Seeds are paired because, for a given seed value, every arm sees the same fixed $40$-image $S_1$ subset and the same seed, so we test differences with paired $t$-tests and correct each family of $12$ columns with Holm's procedure.

\paragraph{Few-shot stress ($S_1$, $40$ labelled images).} DANG-Med v4.1 raises mDice on tiny/camouflaged lesions from $44.74 \pm 3.79\%$ to $51.05 \pm 1.71\%$ ($+6.31$~pp, $p=0.014$; Holm-adjusted $p=0.055$) and on synthetic adverse endoscopy from $45.59 \pm 4.02\%$ to $55.82 \pm 1.17\%$ ($+10.23$~pp, $p=0.005$). Boundary F1 rises by $11.9$--$14.4$~pp on all three few-shot cohorts and these gains survive correction. On the four PraNet external sets mDice rises by $5.27$~pp ($p=0.038$, not significant after correction) while its seed-to-seed SD falls from $3.84$ to $0.34$~pp. The few-shot in-domain score also improves ($86.65 \pm 0.82\% \to 87.90 \pm 0.70\%$, $p=0.011$).

\paragraph{Full training ($800$ labelled images).} DANG-Med v4.1 reaches $78.88 \pm 1.50\%$ mDice on ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($+6.33$~pp, $p=0.005$), $81.82 \pm 0.64\%$ on the PraNet external macro ($+3.16$~pp, $p=0.003$) and reduces PraNet-external HD95 from $35.13 \pm 1.92$ to $28.84 \pm 1.04$~px ($p=0.008$). In domain the models are indistinguishable ($92.95 \pm 0.31\%$ vs.\ $92.92 \pm 0.14\%$ on Kvasir-Test, $p=0.81$), so the gains are not bought with in-domain accuracy. On full-data $S_3$, mDice rises from $55.78 \pm 3.63\%$ to $67.44 \pm 1.11\%$ ($p=0.003$) with the largest per-family gains on low-light ($+18.7$~pp) and combined-severe frames ($+23.0$~pp).

\paragraph{Untouched confirmation cohort.} PolypGen-Extended ($n=974$) was not inspected during design. DANG-Med v4.1 improves its macro mDice from $78.71 \pm 0.76\%$ to $80.09 \pm 0.45\%$ ($+1.38$~pp, $p=0.009$). Because $74$ of these frames share \texttt{C4} video sequences with PolypGen-Dev, we also evaluate the sequence-disjoint subset ($n=900$): its micro mDice improves from $83.33 \pm 0.61\%$ to $84.50 \pm 0.37\%$ ($+1.18$~pp, $p=0.004$). The sequence-disjoint \emph{macro} average does not move ($78.48\%$ vs.\ $78.51\%$), but it weights the $20$ remaining \texttt{C4} frames as one sixth of the score and is dominated by their noise.

\paragraph{A second backbone alone does not help.} The SafeConcat control adds the same frozen DINOv2-Base and Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA as DANG-Med but fuses by concatenation into an FPN. In the few-shot regime it is indistinguishable from the baseline ($44.92\%$ vs.\ $44.74\%$ on $S_1\times S_2$; $46.33\%$ vs.\ $45.59\%$ on $S_1\times S_3$); it helps only on full-data $S_3$ ($63.26\%$ vs.\ $55.78\%$). The gain of DANG-Med therefore comes from how the semantic stream is used, not from its presence.

\subsection{Which Components Matter}
\label{sec:ablation}
Figure~\ref{fig:deltas} separates the two parts of the design. The no-stem arm (C: ProtoDPT + PASSE + uncertainty strip decoder) already captures almost all of the improvement over the baseline: $+5.32$~pp on $S_1\times S_2$, $+11.65$~pp on $S_1\times S_3$, $+4.95$~pp on $S_1\to$PraNet and $+4.94$~pp on full-data ETIS. Adding the Retinex chromatic stem and DINO-conditioned style calibration on top (F $-$ C) changes no mDice cohort significantly after correction. Its one uncorrected mDice effect is a \emph{loss} on NBI-shifted frames ($64.34\%$ vs.\ $68.66\%$, $-4.32$~pp, $p=0.008$), and it raises $S_1\times S_3$ HD95 by $7.32$~px ($p=0.012$); the only favourable uncorrected effect is $+3.29$~pp Boundary F1 on $S_1\times S_2$ ($p=0.022$). A plausible reading (an inference we did not test) is that the stem's EMA standardisation, fitted only to white-light Kvasir statistics, maps the green-dominant NBI palette onto a white-light-like distribution that the prototype head has not seen.

The single-seed Round~9 arm \texttt{R9d}, which keeps the stem, style calibration, PASSE and the strip decoder but drops the prototype head, points the same way: on seed~$42$ it scores $53.43\%$ on $S_1\times S_3$ against $59.55\%$ for \texttt{R9c} (Table~\ref{tab:s1_stress}). Because \texttt{R9d} ran on one seed and also loses the auxiliary prototype loss, we treat this as supporting rather than decisive evidence that the prototype path is the active ingredient. A clean factorial over PASSE, the prototype loss and the strip decoder at five seeds is left for future work.

\subsection{Single-Seed Breakdown and the Round~8 Reference}
Tables~\ref{tab:s1_stress} and~\ref{tab:full_ood} give the complete metric set (mDice, mIoU, BF1, HD95) for seed~$42$, including the single-seed \texttt{R9d} arm and the earlier Round~8 suite. Seed~$42$ is the baseline's weakest few-shot draw ($39.00\%$ $S_1\times S_2$ mDice vs.\ $43.52$--$48.49\%$ on seeds $43$--$46$), so its gains (e.g.\ $+10.79$~pp on $S_1\times S_2$) are roughly twice the five-seed paired means and should not be quoted as the effect size; Table~\ref{tab:multiseed} is the primary result.

Round~8 differs from Round~9/10 in several factors at once ($10\%$ vs.\ $5\%$ few-shot budget, the loss, the absence of PASSE, and a looser deduplication threshold), so it is not an ablation. Its flagship \texttt{R8e} trained the stem with online NBI, low-light, glare+blur and combined corruptions (\texttt{aug\_prob=0.55}) that mirror the $S_3$ test families. Its $S_3$ scores ($64.79\%$ few-shot, $73.87\%$ full, marked $^\dagger$) are therefore corruption-matched rather than zero-shot, and serve only as a reference for what in-family training achieves.

\subsection{HD95 Penalty Sensitivity and Empty Predictions}
\label{sec:hd95}
Our metric code assigns a fixed HD95 of $0.25\sqrt{352^2+352^2}\approx124.45$~px when a prediction is empty and the ground truth is not. This penalty is smaller than the HD95 of $10.9\%$ ($8{,}524$ of $78{,}143$) of non-empty predictions across all four arms, five seeds and all cohorts, so an empty mask can score a better HD95 than a mask with a distant false-positive blob. Empty-prediction rates are similar across arms: on $S_1\times S_2$ $28.52\%$ for DANG-Med v4.1 vs.\ $27.81\%$ for the baseline (per seed $[30.3, 29.7, 28.4, 30.0, 24.2]$ vs.\ $[25.5, 24.5, 28.7, 34.2, 26.1]\%$), and on $S_1\to$PraNet $13.45\%$ vs.\ $14.77\%$. Recomputing HD95 from the per-image logs with half-diagonal ($248.90$~px) and full-diagonal ($497.80$~px) penalties gives, on $S_1\times S_2$, $100.17 \pm 4.50$ vs.\ $107.37 \pm 8.56$~px and $171.15 \pm 10.67$ vs.\ $176.58 \pm 14.30$~px, and on $S_1\to$PraNet (macro) $62.29 \pm 1.64$ vs.\ $72.29 \pm 14.22$~px and $87.76 \pm 0.75$ vs.\ $99.34 \pm 14.36$~px. The direction of the HD95 difference holds under every penalty, but its size shrinks as the penalty grows, and on these two cohorts it is not significant under the default penalty ($p=0.135$ and $p=0.258$). We therefore base our claims on mDice and Boundary F1 and treat HD95 as secondary.

\subsection{Efficiency}
At inference (batch size $1$, $352\times352$, FP16, NVIDIA T4) DANG-Med v4.1 runs at $17.4$~FPS with $43.32$M trainable of $129.90$M total parameters, $86.58$M of them frozen in DINOv2-Base. The baseline runs at $91.9$~FPS with $30.96$M parameters (Table~\ref{tab:full_ood}). DANG-Med is real-time for frame-by-frame review at typical $25$--$30$~Hz only with frame skipping or a faster GPU; we did not optimise for throughput.

\section{Limitations, Ethical Compliance, and Reproducibility}
We note seven limitations.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single-centre training and a fixed few-shot subset.} All models are trained on Kvasir-SEG only ($n=800$ full; $n=40$ in $S_1$), unlike the common PraNet split that also uses CVC-ClinicDB, so that ClinicDB remains an unseen centre. The $40$-image subset is fixed across seeds, so Table~\ref{tab:multiseed} captures optimisation variance but not the variance of drawing a different $40$ images.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive design selection.} PASSE thresholds, far-field dilation radii and other hyperparameters were developed over Rounds~1--9 while inspecting PolypGen-Dev, $S_2$, $S_3$ and ETIS. Those cohorts are therefore not fully held out. PolypGen-Extended ($n=974$) was not used for design and is our confirmation cohort; its gains are smaller ($+1.2$ to $+1.4$~pp) than on the inspected cohorts.
  \item \textbf{The stem is a negative result.} Under the zero-shot protocol (\texttt{aug\_prob=0.0}) the Retinex chromatic stem with style calibration does not improve any cohort significantly and reduces NBI mDice by $4.3$~pp (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). A deployment would use the no-stem model, which is also $\sim 0.5$M parameters smaller.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic adverse cohort and derived slices.} $S_3$ applies deterministic photometric and optical corruptions to PolypGen-Dev frames; it is not a set of paired physical NBI/WLI acquisitions. $S_2$ and $S_3$ are diagnostic slices of PolypGen-Dev, ETIS and ColonDB rather than independent patient cohorts.
  \item \textbf{Statistical power.} Five seeds give a paired $t$-test with four degrees of freedom. We report raw and Holm-adjusted significance; several raw $p<0.05$ effects (e.g.\ $S_1\times S_2$ mDice) do not survive correction, and HD95 differences on two cohorts are not significant.
  \item \textbf{Prototype supervision is confounded with the prototype head.} Arms with the prototype head also receive the auxiliary loss $0.35\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}$, so their gains reflect both.
  \item \textbf{No retrained literature baselines and no qualitative predictions.} We compare controlled internal architectures and do not retrain PraNet, Polyp-PVT or other published models under our $5\%$ split. Model checkpoints were not retained, so Figure~\ref{fig:examples} shows inputs, ground truth and logged per-image Dice rather than predicted masks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
DANG-Med couples a CNN stream with a frozen DINOv2 stream through a prototype margin head, a prototype-anchored support envelope and an uncertainty-guided strip decoder. Over five paired seeds it improves Boundary F1 by $12$--$14$~pp on every few-shot stress cohort, raises mDice on synthetic adverse endoscopy by $10$~pp and on ETIS-LaribPolypDB by $6$~pp, and holds in-domain accuracy. Two controls locate the gain: concatenating the same foundation features into an FPN does not help in the few-shot regime, and removing the Retinex stem and style calibration costs nothing. The next steps are a five-seed factorial over PASSE, the prototype loss and the strip decoder; evaluation on paired physical NBI/WLI acquisitions; resampling the few-shot subset; and retraining published polyp models under the same protocol.
