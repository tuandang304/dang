\begin{abstract}
Deep convolutional and vision transformer networks have achieved strong in-domain accuracy on curated colonoscopy benchmarks such as Kvasir-SEG ($\sim 93\%$ mDice), yet their segmentation reliability degrades under real-world clinical stress conditions: (i) severe label scarcity when adapting to new clinical centers ($5\%\text{--}10\%$ labeled frames), (ii) natural tiny ($<1.5\%$ mucosal area) and flat low-contrast camouflaged polyps across unseen imaging systems (e.g., ETIS-LaribPolypDB), and (iii) adverse endoscopic acquisition shifts including Narrow-Band Imaging (NBI) spectral emphasis, distal-lumen low-light falloff, and wet mucosal specular glare with motion defocus. To address these clinical failure modes on a single GPU, we propose \textbf{DANG-Med} (\textit{Dual-Stream Attention-Augmented Network with Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support and Gated Directional Strip Alignment}), a hybrid architecture coupling a local multi-scale ConvNeXt-Tiny hierarchy with a frozen DINOv2-Base foundation backbone ($85.95\text{M}$ frozen parameters) adapted via Frequency-Routed Mixture-of-Experts Convolutional LoRA (\textbf{Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA}). DANG-Med introduces four architectural pillars: (1) a \textbf{4-Scale Learned DPT Multi-Layer Reassembler} with a \textbf{Dual-Prototype and Image-Adaptive Self-Support Margin Head} that separates polyp versus mucosa token manifolds under $40$-image few-shot supervision; (2) a \textbf{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)} that suppresses isolated far-field false-positive background specks while reinforcing diminutive lesion cores; (3) a \textbf{Multi-Scale Homomorphic Retinex Chromatic Stem} paired with a \textbf{DINOv2-Conditioned Low-Frequency Style Calibrator}; and (4) an \textbf{Uncertainty-Guided Reverse-Attention Directional Strip Decoder} (\textbf{FastAxialStripSSM}) modulated by normalized Bernoulli boundary variance $4p(1-p)$. We evaluate two distinct protocols across deduplicated in-domain, multi-center out-of-distribution (PolypGen 6-center dev/extended, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-ColonDB, CVC-300, ETIS-LaribPolypDB), and synthetic adverse stress cohorts: (A) our primary \textbf{Round~9/10 zero-shot stem protocol} ($5\%$ few-shot, $n=40$), where all models share the same primary segmentation objective and no adverse synthetic corruptions are used during training; and (B) our \textbf{Round~8 adverse-trained stem protocol} ($10\%$ few-shot, $n=80$). Under the Round~9 Seed~42 benchmark, \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 (\texttt{R9e})} improves over the single-stream ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN baseline (\texttt{R9a}) by \textbf{$+10.79$~pp ($+27.68\%$ relative) mDice}, \textbf{$+12.88$~pp ($+42.20\%$ relative) mIoU}, \textbf{$+22.68$~pp ($+234.38\%$ relative) Boundary F1}, and \textbf{$-26.85\%$ HD95 error} on few-shot tiny/camouflaged lesions ($S_1\times S_2$, $n=310$), and by \textbf{$+11.86$~pp ($+19.70\%$ relative) mDice}, \textbf{$+29.93\%$ relative mIoU}, \textbf{$+121.76\%$ relative Boundary F1}, and \textbf{$-43.88\%$ HD95 error} on few-shot external generalization ($S_1\to\text{PraNet-Ext}$, $n=696$). On full-data training ($n=800$), zero-shot \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 (\texttt{R9e})} reaches \textbf{$80.78\%$ mDice on ETIS-LaribPolypDB} ($+6.22$~pp over shared-loss \texttt{R9a}, $-16.77\%$ HD95), while adverse-trained \textbf{DANG-Med v4 (\texttt{R8e})} reaches \textbf{$81.53\%$ mDice on ETIS-LaribPolypDB} ($+13.92$~pp / $+20.58\%$ relative over standard baseline \texttt{R8a}) and \textbf{$73.87\%$ mDice on Adverse Endoscopy ($S_3$, $n=272$)}. Multi-seed evaluation (`R10a`--`R10e`, Seeds 42--46) confirms consistent gains and substantially reduced cross-seed variance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, and early endoscopic detection and resection of adenomatous and sessile serrated polyps during optical colonoscopy significantly reduces CRC incidence~\citep{jha2020kvasir,ali2023polypgen}. Automated pixel-level polyp segmentation provides computer-aided delineation (CADe/CADx) of lesion margins during withdrawal. However, a substantial gap persists between in-domain benchmark saturation and multi-center clinical deployment. On standard in-domain white-light colonoscopy splits such as Kvasir-SEG~\citep{jha2020kvasir}, modern hierarchical convolutional networks (e.g., ConvNeXt-Tiny~\citep{liu2022convnet} with Feature Pyramid Networks~\citep{lin2017fpn}) already achieve $92.5\%\text{--}93.1\%$ mean Dice (mDice). Under such saturated in-domain conditions, architectural modifications yield modest differences ($<0.5\text{--}1.0$~percentage points), obscuring whether a proposed model genuinely improves out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness.

In clinical practice, endoscopic segmentation systems degrade across three compounding stress axes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Label-Scarce Multi-Center Adaptation ($S_1$):} Annotating pixel-accurate polyp contours requires expert gastroenterologists. When training under a strict $5\%\text{--}10\%$ annotation budget ($40\text{--}80$ labeled images), standard CNNs overfit to source-hospital background textures and suffer severe boundary degradation on external centers ($\text{Boundary F1} < 20\%$, $\text{HD95} > 80\,\text{px}$).
  \item \textbf{Natural Tiny and Low-Contrast Camouflaged Lesions ($S_2$):} Flat Paris~0-IIa/IIb adenomas and diminutive polyps occupying $<1.5\%$ of the endoscopic field of view (FOV) or exhibiting low RGB contrast ($<0.085$) against surrounding vascular mucosa are frequently missed (elevated empty-prediction rates) or fragmented by stride-$16/32$ feature pooling, particularly on external cohorts such as ETIS-LaribPolypDB~\citep{silva2014toward} and CVC-ColonDB~\citep{tajbakhsh2015automated}.
  \item \textbf{Adverse Endoscopic Acquisition Shifts ($S_3$):} Endoscopic procedures frequently switch between White-Light Imaging (WLI) and Narrow-Band Imaging (NBI)-like spectral shifts, encounter distal-lumen illumination falloff ($\gamma > 2.2$ with sensor noise), and suffer from wet mucosal specular highlights coupled with motion/defocus blur.
\end{enumerate}

While Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2} and SAM~\citep{kirillov2023segment} offer strong semantic invariance across domains, their coarse patch grids ($14\times 14$ stride) lack fine sub-pixel boundary localization. Conversely, naive feature concatenation between a CNN encoder and a ViT backbone can degrade high-resolution CNN edge features when ViT patch tokens are bilinearly upsampled across lesion boundaries, and sequential 2D state-space scans (SS2D~\citep{liu2024vmamba}) incur substantial Python/CUDA kernel launch overhead on multi-scale feature pyramids.

In this work, we present \textbf{DANG-Med}, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:arch}. Our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Auditable Multi-Center Deduplication Hygiene and Clinical Stress Evaluation:} We implement an automated perceptual-hash and MD5 deduplication pipeline (\texttt{HYGIENE\_MANIFEST\_JSON}) across Kvasir-SEG, $6$-center PolypGen~\citep{ali2023polypgen} (Dev $n=258$ and Extended $n=974$ in Round~9/10; $n=272$ and $n=967$ in Round~8), all $4$ official PraNet external benchmarks~\citep{fan2020pranet} ($n=696$ after removing $2$ exact/FOV duplicates against Kvasir: CVC-300 $n=60$, CVC-ClinicDB $n=61$, CVC-ColonDB $n=379$, ETIS-LaribPolypDB $n=196$), and derived stress tracks ($S_1$ $5\%/10\%$ few-shot, $S_2$ pooled tiny/camouflaged lesions $n=310/315$, and $S_3$ synthetic adverse acquisitions $n=258/272$).
  \item \textbf{4-Scale DPT Reassembly, Dual-Prototype Self-Support, and PASSE Envelope:} We project multi-depth DINOv2 tokens into an $L_2$-normalized prototype hypersphere combining orthogonally initialized $K$-prototype cosine margins, training-gated ground-truth EMA support anchors, and test-time image-adaptive self-support centroids. Crucially, we introduce the \textbf{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)}, which forms a dilated semantic support envelope to suppress far-field false-positive background specks (reducing $\text{HD95}$) while reinforcing low-contrast tiny polyp cores.
  \item \textbf{Retinex Chromatic Stem and DINO-Conditioned Style Calibration:} We design a differentiable homomorphic Retinex stem (\texttt{RetinexChromaticStem}) that performs specular highlight inpainting and multi-scale illumination division gated by an anomaly detector against source white-light EMA statistics (evaluated both without adverse-corruption training in Round~9/10 and with online endoscopic shift synthesis in Round~8), complemented by DINOv2-conditioned low-frequency style calibration at strides $4$ and $8$.
  \item \textbf{Directional Strip Alignment and Uncertainty-Guided Reverse-Attention Decoder:} We formulate \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM} using fused cuDNN horizontal ($1\times 11, 7\times 1$), vertical ($11\times 1, 1\times 7$), and dilated ($3\times 3, d=3$) depthwise strip operators modulated by normalized Bernoulli boundary variance $u = 4p(1-p)$ and outer morphological ring halos.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Polyp Segmentation and Boundary-Aware Decoders}
Deep polyp segmentation architectures have progressed from U-Net~\citep{ronneberger2015unet} and UNet++~\citep{zhou2018unetplusplus} to reverse-attention and boundary-aware networks. PraNet~\citep{fan2020pranet} introduced parallel partial decoders and iterative reverse attention to mine boundary cues from Res2Net backbones. CaraNet~\citep{lou2022caranet} extended reverse attention with axial context and channel-wise feature pyramid modules for small medical objects. Subsequent Transformer-based architectures such as Polyp-PVT~\citep{dong2021polyp} and TransFuse~\citep{zhang2021transfuse} paired or replaced CNN encoders with Pyramid Vision Transformers to improve multi-center generalization on CVC-ColonDB and ETIS-LaribPolypDB. Unlike dual-branch models that fuse features only at coarse $1/16$ and $1/32$ scales with unconstrained bilinear upsampling, DANG-Med performs 4-scale learned DPT depth mixing with content-aware edge-snapped upsampling and explicit spatial support gating at stride~$4$ ($88\times 88$).

\subsection{Vision Foundation Models and Parameter-Efficient Adaptation}
Self-supervised Vision Transformers pretrained via discriminative self-distillation, notably DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2}, exhibit strong part-level semantic consistency across domains, while promptable foundation models such as SAM~\citep{kirillov2023segment} and its medical adaptations require either bounding-box prompts or heavy decoder fine-tuning. Adapting VFMs to autonomous dense medical segmentation while preventing overfitting on small clinical datasets ($N \le 800$) benefits from parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) such as LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} and Dense Prediction Transformers (DPT)~\citep{ranftl2021vision}. We freeze $100\%$ of the DINOv2-Base backbone ($85.95\text{M}$ parameters; $86.58\text{M}$ total frozen buffers) and inject lightweight Frequency-Routed Mixture-of-Experts Convolutional LoRA (\textbf{Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA}) adapters into blocks $8\text{--}11$, decomposing spatial tokens into low-frequency context and high-frequency edge energy routed across dilation rates $d \in \{1,2,3\}$.

\subsection{State-Space and Directional Strip Operators in Medical Vision}
Structured State-Space Sequence Models (S4, Mamba~\citep{gu2023mamba}) and 2D visual extensions (VMamba~\citep{liu2024vmamba}, U-Mamba~\citep{ma2024umamba}) model long-range spatial dependencies. However, executing recursive 4-directional 1D scans in Python across multi-scale 2D feature pyramids incurs substantial kernel launch overhead. Motivated by the connection between truncated state-space convolutional kernels and directional strip convolutions, our \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM} captures horizontal, vertical, and dilated contextual dependencies using fused depthwise strip convolutions gated by cross-stream conditioning, achieving $17.4\text{--}20.0$~FPS (batch size $4$, $352\times 352$, FP16 on NVIDIA T4).

\section{Methodology: The DANG-Med Architecture}
Given an input endoscopic RGB image $I \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ ($H = W = 352$), DANG-Med (\texttt{src/dang/model.py}) predicts a dense foreground polyp probability map $\hat{Y} = \sigma(Z) \in [0, 1]^{B \times 1 \times H \times W}$. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the complete pipeline.

\subsection{Retinex Chromatic Stem and DINO-Conditioned Style Calibrator}
Endoscopic frames from external centers or adverse modes exhibit non-uniform illumination and spectral shifts. Let $X_{\text{rgb}} \in [0, 1]^{B \times 3 \times H \times W}$ denote the unnormalized RGB image entering \texttt{RetinexChromaticStem}.
\paragraph{1) Specular Highlight Inpainting:} Mucosal glare saturates all three RGB channels. We detect specular pixels via a smooth minimum-channel gate $w_{\text{glare}} = \sigma(18.0 \cdot (\min_c X_{\text{rgb}} - 0.82))$ and blend them with a $15\times 15$ local average filter $\bar{X}_{15}$:
\begin{equation}
  X_{\text{deg}} = (1 - w_{\text{glare}}) X_{\text{rgb}} + w_{\text{glare}} \bar{X}_{15}.
  \label{eq:deglare}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{2) Multi-Scale Homomorphic Retinex Division:} Approximating image intensity as the product of illumination $L(x)$ and mucosal reflectance $R(x)$, we estimate illumination envelopes $L_{31} = \text{AvgPool}_{31\times 31}(X_{\text{deg}}) + \epsilon$ and $L_{15} = \text{AvgPool}_{15\times 15}(X_{\text{deg}}) + \epsilon$ ($\epsilon = 0.03$) and recover log-reflectance:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{R} = \log(X_{\text{deg}} + \epsilon) - \bigl(0.65 \log L_{31} + 0.35 \log L_{15}\bigr).
  \label{eq:retinex}
\end{equation}
\paragraph{3) Source EMA Standardization and Anomaly Gate:} During training on source Kvasir-SEG images, the stem updates exponential moving average reference buffers $(\mu_{\text{ref}}, \sigma_{\text{ref}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times 3\times 1\times 1}$ (in training mode only; frozen during \texttt{model.eval()}). In our Round~9/10 protocol (`R9d`, `R9e`, `R10d`, `R10e`), `aug\_prob` inside the stem is hard-set to $0.0$ so no synthetic adverse corruptions are generated during training (source-trained with standard photometric jitter, evaluated on unseen $S_3$ synthetic corruptions). In our Round~8 protocol (`R8d`, `R8e`), online endoscopic shift synthesis is enabled during training for comparison. Combining instance-standardized reflectance $\hat{R}$ and global chromaticity $\hat{X}_{\text{deg}}$, the restored image is $X_{\text{rest}} = \text{clip}((0.55 \hat{X}_{\text{deg}} + 0.45 \hat{R}) \sigma_{\text{ref}} + \mu_{\text{ref}}, 0, 1)$. A photometric anomaly score $A_{\text{phot}}$ measures the normalized deviation of $( \mu(X_{\text{deg}}), \sigma(X_{\text{deg}}) )$ from $(\mu_{\text{ref}}, \sigma_{\text{ref}})$ plus glare fraction, gating a learned $3\times 3$ residual refinement branch (with minimum blend floor $0.04$).

At high-resolution ConvNeXt stages $s_4$ ($88\times 88$) and $s_8$ ($44\times 44$), \textbf{DINOFreqStyleCalibrator} decomposes local CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}$ into a $9\times 9$ low-frequency component $F_{\text{low}}$ and high-frequency detail $F_{\text{high}} = F_{\text{loc}} - F_{\text{low}}$, modulating both via affine parameters $(\gamma_{\text{low}}, \beta_{\text{low}}, \gamma_{\text{high}})$ predicted from the global DINOv2 context vector $g_{\text{dino}} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768}$.

\subsection{4-Scale DPT Reassembly and Dual-Prototype Self-Support Margin Head}
We extract spatial token grids $\{T_\ell\}_{\ell \in \{2,5,8,11\}} \subset \mathbb{R}^{B \times 768 \times 25 \times 25}$ from DINOv2-Base (with Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA of rank $r=4$ on blocks $8\text{--}11$) and project each to $D = 192$ channels: $S_\ell = \text{ConvBNAct}_{1\times 1}(T_\ell)$.
\paragraph{4-Scale Learned DPT Reassembler:} We learn a $4\times 4$ depth-mixing matrix $W = \text{Softmax}(\Theta) \in \mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}$ across scales $s \in \{0,1,2,3\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{S}_s = \sum_{\ell=0}^{3} W_{s,\ell} S_\ell, \quad \hat{S}_s = \phi_s\bigl(\text{Up}_s(\tilde{S}_s, F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)})\bigr),
  \label{eq:dpt}
\end{equation}
where scales $s_8$ and $s_{16}$ use content-aware \textbf{Edge-Snapped Semantic Upsampling} (predicting $3\times 3$ local reassembly kernels from CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$, initialized to bilinear interpolation).

\paragraph{Dual-Prototype and Self-Support Margin Head:} We concatenate $[S_1, S_2, S_3]$, project to $D_p = 128$ dimensions, and $L_2$-normalize across channels to obtain unit token embeddings $\hat{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D_p \times 25 \times 25}$. We initialize $K=8$ learnable foreground prototypes $\{p_k^+\}_{k=1}^K$ and $K=8$ background prototypes $\{p_k^-\}_{k=1}^K$ orthogonally, alongside training-gated ground-truth EMA support anchors $(a^+, a^-)$ updated only during training from downsampled mask occupancy ($M_{25} \ge 0.35$ for foreground, $M_{25} \le 0.10$ for background). The prior cosine margin logit at each $25\times 25$ patch $(u,v)$ is:
\begin{align}
  \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) &= \tau \Bigl( \frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, \hat{p}_k^+\rangle} - \frac{1}{\beta}\log\sum_{k=1}^K e^{\beta \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, \hat{p}_k^-\rangle} \Bigr) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + w_{\text{ema}} \cdot 10\bigl(\langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, a^+\rangle - \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, a^-\rangle\bigr) + b.
  \label{eq:proto_prior}
\end{align}
During inference (without ground-truth masks), we compute image-specific self-support centroids $c^+ = \text{Norm}(\sum_{u,v} p_{u,v}^2 \hat{Z}_{u,v})$ and $c^- = \text{Norm}(\sum_{u,v} (1-p_{u,v})^2 \hat{Z}_{u,v})$ where $p_{u,v} = \sigma(\pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v))$, yielding the $25\times 25$ prototype margin map:
\begin{equation}
  \pi_{\text{proto}}(u,v) = \pi_{\text{prior}}(u,v) + w_{\text{ss}} \cdot 8\bigl(\langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, c^+\rangle - \langle \hat{Z}_{u,v}, c^-\rangle\bigr).
  \label{eq:proto_total}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE)}
Recall-enhancing prototype priors reduce missed polyps (empty predictions) on diminutive and adverse images, but can allow isolated high-frequency CNN texture noise far from the true polyp to cross the $0.5$ threshold, inflating $\text{HD95}$. To resolve this trade-off, \textbf{PASSE} constructs a $25\times 25$ semantic probability map $P_{\text{sem}} = \sigma(0.5 M_4^{\uparrow 25} + 0.5 \pi_{\text{proto}})$ from the coarsest decoder stage logit $M_4$ and prototype logit $\pi_{\text{proto}}$ (falling back to $P_{\text{sem}} = \sigma(M_4^{\uparrow 25})$ when the prototype head is disabled, as in variant \texttt{R9d}), and computes a dilated smooth support envelope:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{E}_{\text{env}} = \text{AvgPool}_{3\times 3}\bigl(\text{MaxPool}_{5\times 5}(P_{\text{sem}})\bigr) \in [0,1]^{B\times 1\times 25\times 25}.
  \label{eq:passe_env}
\end{equation}
Upsampling $\mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}$ and $P_{\text{sem}}$ to stride~$4$ ($88\times 88$), PASSE modulates the stride-$4$ decoder logit $Z_{88}$ via two spatial terms:
\begin{equation}
  Z_{88}^{\text{PASSE}} = Z_{88} - 2.20\,\frac{\text{ReLU}(0.22 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{env}}^{\uparrow 88})}{0.22} + 1.20\,\text{ReLU}(P_{\text{sem}}^{\uparrow 88} - 0.38).
  \label{eq:passe_mod}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Uncertainty-Guided Directional Strip Decoder}
At each pyramid scale $s \in \{s_8, s_{16}, s_{32}\}$, \textbf{GDSSAFusion} aligns local CNN features $F_{\text{loc}}^{(s)}$ and semantic features $\hat{S}_s$ using \textbf{FastAxialStripSSM}, a gated finite-kernel directional strip operator:
\begin{equation}
  \text{StripSSM}(U, C) = U + g_h \odot \mathcal{K}_{7\times 1}(\mathcal{K}_{1\times 11}(U)) + g_v \odot \mathcal{K}_{1\times 7}(\mathcal{K}_{11\times 1}(U)) + g_d \odot \mathcal{K}_{3\times 3, d=3}(U),
  \label{eq:stripssm}
\end{equation}
where $(g_h, g_v, g_d) = \sigma(\text{Conv}_{1\times 1}([U, C]))$ are input-conditioned directional gates and $\mathcal{K}$ denotes depthwise strip convolutions. In the top-down decoder ($P_4 \to P_3 \to P_2 \to P_1$), each \textbf{UncertaintyStripDecoderStage} computes from the coarser stage's logit $M_{s+1}^{\uparrow}$ three spatial priors: (i) foreground probability $A_{\text{fg}} = \sigma(M_{s+1}^{\uparrow})$, (ii) normalized Bernoulli boundary variance $U_s = 4 A_{\text{fg}}(1 - A_{\text{fg}}) \in [0,1]$ which peaks at $1.0$ along ambiguous borders ($A_{\text{fg}} = 0.5$), and (iii) an outer morphological ring halo $R_s = \text{clip}(\text{MaxPool}_{5\times 5}(A_{\text{fg}}) - A_{\text{fg}}, 0, 1)$, followed by A2BD Sobel-$L_1$ edge refinement.

\subsection{Training Objective and Variant Configuration Matrix}
In Round~9 and Round~10 (`R9a`--`R9e`, `R10a`--`R10e`), all arms use the exact same loss implementation \texttt{compute\_total\_loss} (\texttt{src/dang/train.py}, lines 103--140):
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{\tilde{w}}(Z, Y) + 0.25\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{\tilde{w},\alpha(a)}(Z, Y) + 0.10\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z, Y) \nonumber \\
  &\quad + 0.25\sum_{j=2}^4 \frac{1}{3}\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{\tilde{w}}(M_j, Y) + 0.35\,\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}(\pi_{\text{proto}}, Y_{25}),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{align}
where $a = 100 \cdot \bar{Y}$ is the ground-truth lesion area percentage, $w_i = \text{clip}((3.0 / (a_i + 0.35))^{0.40}, 1.0, 2.4)$ is normalized per batch via $\tilde{w}_i = w_i / \bar{w}$, $\alpha_{\text{fp}}(a) = 0.45 + 0.15\sigma((a - 1.5)/0.5)$ and $\beta_{\text{fn}}(a) = 1 - \alpha_{\text{fp}}(a)$ adapt Tversky supervision by lesion area, and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{far}}(Z, Y) = \frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_x \sigma(Z_x) \cdot \frac{1}{4}\sum_{r \in \{4,8,14,20\}} (1 - \text{Dilate}_r(Y)_x)$ penalizes far-field false positives across all five arms. The auxiliary prototype supervision term $0.35\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}} = 0.35(\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE+Dice}}^{\tilde{w}}(\pi_{\text{proto}}, Y_{25}) + 0.25\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{\tilde{w},\alpha(a)}(\pi_{\text{proto}}, Y_{25}))$ is active whenever the architecture includes the prototype head ($\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=1$ in \texttt{R9c}, \texttt{R9e}, \texttt{R8c}, \texttt{R8e}; $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$ in \texttt{R9a}, \texttt{R9b}, \texttt{R9d}, \texttt{R8a}, \texttt{R8b}, \texttt{R8d}). Specifically, the five Round~9 arms are configured as follows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9a} (Single-Stream Baseline, run \texttt{f328e771}, commit \texttt{e1379f6}):} ConvNeXt-Tiny + FPN decoder ($30.96\text{M}$ trainable / $30.96\text{M}$ total params), trained with Eq.~(\ref{eq:loss}) ($\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9b} (Dual-Stream SafeConcat Control, run \texttt{e770b25c}, commit \texttt{946256c}):} ConvNeXt-Tiny + frozen DINOv2-Base with Freq-MoE Conv-LoRA + SafeConcat fusion + FPN decoder ($33.57\text{M}$ trainable / $120.15\text{M}$ total params, $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=0$).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{R9c} (DANG-v4.1 ProtoDPT + PASSE + UncStrip, No Stem, run \texttt{f676224f}, commit \texttt{1218ebb}):} Enables \texttt{use\_proto\_dpt=true}, \texttt{use\_passe\_envelope=true}, \texttt{fusion\_mode=safe\_gd\_ssa\_v2}, \texttt{decoder\_mode=uncertainty\_strip\_ssm}, \texttt{use\_retinex\_stem=false}, \texttt{use\_style\_calib=false} ($42.80\text{M}$ trainable / $129.38\text{M}$ total params, $\mathbb{I}[\text{proto}]=1$).

\section{Experimental Setup and Data Hygiene}
\subsection{Datasets, Deduplication Hygiene, and Evaluation Cohorts}
Public composite polyp repositories (e.g., \texttt{combo\_polyp} / PolypGen) contain UUID-named Kvasir-SEG images in their raw splits and occasional near-duplicate frames. We enforce an automated deduplication pipeline (\texttt{src/dang/data.py}, logged via \texttt{HYGIENE\_MANIFEST\_JSON}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{In-Domain Kvasir-SEG ($n=1{,}000$ raw):} Shuffled (\texttt{seed=20260101}) into \textbf{Train-Full ($n=800$)}, \textbf{Train-$S_1$ Few-Shot ($5\%$, $n=40$ in R9/R10; $10\%$, $n=80$ in R8)}, \textbf{Val ($n=95$)}, and \textbf{ID-Test ($n=90$)}, where any validation or test image with $64$-bit perceptual difference hash distance $\text{dHash}_{64} \le 4$ to any training image is dropped ($5$ in Val, $10$ in ID-Test).
  \item \textbf{PolypGen 6-Center Out-of-Distribution (\texttt{C1}--\texttt{C6})~\citep{ali2023polypgen}:} Kvasir UUID filenames ($204$ in Dev, $796$ in Extended), empty masks ($30$ in Dev, $86$ in Extended), exact MD5 duplicates, and frames with $\min_{x \in \text{Kvasir}} \text{dHash}_{64} \le 4$ ($14$ in Dev, $78$ in Extended) are removed, yielding \textbf{PolypGen-Dev ($n=258$ in R9/R10; $n=272$ in R8 prior to the $\text{dHash}_{64}\le 4$ filter)} and \textbf{PolypGen-Extended Holdout ($n=974$ in R9/R10 after also filtering $47$ Dev near-duplicates and $49$ intra-Extended near-duplicates; $n=967$ in R8)}. We note that $74$ frames in PolypGen-Extended share \texttt{C4} video sequence prefixes with Dev; we therefore also track sequence-disjoint subsets in our logs.
  \item \textbf{PraNet Official External Benchmarks ($n=696$ total across 4 datasets)~\citep{fan2020pranet}:} After removing $2$ frames with exact/FOV-cropped $\text{dHash}_{64} \le 1$ overlap against Kvasir-SEG ($1$ in CVC-ClinicDB, $1$ in CVC-ColonDB), we evaluate: \textbf{CVC-300 ($n=60$)}, \textbf{CVC-ClinicDB ($n=61$)}, \textbf{CVC-ColonDB ($n=379$)}, and \textbf{ETIS-LaribPolypDB ($n=196$)}.
  \item \textbf{Derived Clinical Stress Track $S_2$ (Natural Tiny \& Camouflaged Polyps, $n=310$ in R9/R10; $n=315$ in R8):} Pooled across PolypGen-Dev, ETIS-LaribPolypDB, and CVC-ColonDB for natural lesions with area $<1.5\%$ of FOV (\texttt{S2\_Tiny\_LT1.5pct}, $n=188$) or area $<3.0\%$ with local boundary RGB contrast $<0.085$ (\texttt{S2\_Camouflaged\_LowContrast}, $n=122$). Because $S_2$ and $S_3$ are derived from the external cohorts above to isolate specific clinical failure modes, they are diagnostic stress slices rather than statistically independent patient cohorts.
  \item \textbf{Derived Clinical Stress Track $S_3$ (Synthetic Adverse Endoscopy, $n=258$ in R9/R10; $n=272$ in R8):} Deterministic transformations of PolypGen-Dev frames across $4$ regimes: \texttt{Adverse\_NBI\_Spectral}, \texttt{Adverse\_LowLight\_Lumen} ($\gamma=2.55$ + vignette + shot noise), \texttt{Adverse\_Specular\_Glare\_Blur}, and \texttt{Adverse\_Combined\_Severe}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Implementation Details and Evaluation Metrics}
All models are trained at $352\times 352$ resolution ($350\times 350 = 25\times 25$ patches for DINOv2-Base) with batch size $16$, AdamW ($\text{lr}_{\text{cnn}} = 1.6\times 10^{-4}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{new}} = 4.0\times 10^{-4}$, $\text{lr}_{\text{peft}} = 1.0\times 10^{-3}$; weight decay $10^{-4}$ on CNN/head parameters and $0.0$ on PEFT/bias/normalization parameters), cosine annealing with warmup, EMA decay $0.992$, and FP16 mixed precision on NVIDIA T4 GPUs. Track~$S_1$ trains for $40$~epochs ($\sim 3.4\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9a}, $\sim 11.8\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9e}), and Track~Full trains for $20$~epochs ($\sim 20.4\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9a}, $\sim 72\text{--}80\,\text{s/epoch}$ on \texttt{R9e}). Checkpoint selection uses Kvasir-Val mDice exclusively (\texttt{model.eval()} never receives ground-truth masks).

At $352\times 352$ resolution with threshold $0.5$ (\texttt{src/dang/metrics.py}), we report \textbf{mDice ($\%$)}, \textbf{mIoU ($\%$)}, \textbf{Boundary F1 ($\%$)} ($1\text{-px}$ morphological contour with $2\text{-px}$ dilation tolerance), and \textbf{95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance ($\text{HD95}$, $\text{px}$)}. When a prediction mask is empty while the ground-truth mask is non-empty (or vice versa), $\text{BF1}=0$ and our implementation assigns a fixed finite quarter-diagonal penalty $\text{HD95}_{\text{empty}} = 0.25\sqrt{352^2+352^2} \approx 124.45\,\text{px}$; we also log non-empty-only Hausdorff distance (\texttt{hd95\_non\_empty}) and empty-prediction miss rates (\texttt{ep}) per cohort.

\subsection{Performance on Label-Scarce and Compounding Stress Benchmarks ($S_1, S_1\times S_2, S_1\times S_3$)}
Table~\ref{tab:s1_stress} and Figure~\ref{fig:stress_bars} present results under the few-shot clinical adaptation track ($S_1$). When trained on $5\%$ of Kvasir-SEG ($n=40$ images, fixed subset across R9/R10 arms), the single-stream ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN baseline (\texttt{R9a}) scores $85.89\%$ mDice on in-domain Kvasir-Test ($n=90$), but degrades substantially on out-of-distribution and compounding stress cohorts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item On \textbf{$S_1 \times S_2$ (Few-Shot Tiny \& Camouflaged Polyps, $n=310$)}, \texttt{R9a} drops to \textbf{$39.00\%$ mDice}, \textbf{$30.53\%$ mIoU}, \textbf{$9.67\%$ Boundary F1}, and \textbf{$87.82\,\text{px}$ HD95} (with an empty-prediction miss rate of $25.48\%$ and non-empty $\text{HD95}=75.29\,\text{px}$). \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 (\texttt{R9e})} achieves \textbf{$49.80\%$ mDice ($+10.79$~pp / $+27.68\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$43.42\%$ mIoU ($+12.88$~pp / $+42.20\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$32.35\%$ Boundary F1 ($+22.68$~pp / $+234.38\%$ relative)}, and reduces \textbf{HD95 to $64.24\,\text{px}$ ($-23.58\,\text{px}$ / $-26.85\%$ relative)}, exceeding $+26\%$ relative improvement across all four metrics simultaneously.
  \item On \textbf{$S_1 \to$ PraNet-External Macro ($4$ unseen datasets, $n=696$)}, \texttt{R9a} scores $60.20\%$ mDice, $49.80\%$ mIoU, $19.97\%$ BF1, and $83.04\,\text{px}$ HD95. \textbf{DANG-Med v4.1 (\texttt{R9e})} improves performance to \textbf{$72.06\%$ mDice ($+11.86$~pp / $+19.70\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$64.71\%$ mIoU ($+14.90$~pp / $+29.93\%$ relative)}, \textbf{$44.29\%$ BF1 ($+24.32$~pp / $+121.76\%$ relative)}, and reduces \textbf{HD95 to $46.60\,\text{px}$ ($-36.44\,\text{px}$ / $-43.88\%$ relative)}, exceeding $+19\%$ relative improvement across all four metrics. On the CVC-300 subset ($n=60$) under $5\%$ labels, \texttt{R9e} improves from $57.98\%$ (\texttt{R9a}) to $85.91\%$ mDice ($+27.93$~pp / $+48.18\%$ relative).

\section{Limitations, Ethical Compliance, and Reproducibility}
To ensure transparent scientific reporting in accordance with IEEE Access standards, we explicitly note five methodological limitations and scope conditions identified during our cross-harness audit:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Domain Scope and Fixed Subset Sampling:} All models are trained on single-center white-light colonoscopy images from Kvasir-SEG ($n=800$ Full; fixed $n=40$ subset in R9/R10 $S_1$). Generalization to capsule endoscopy, upper GI gastroscopy, or prospective clinical video streams requires further multi-center validation. Moreover, because the $40$-image $S_1$ subset is held fixed across seeds to isolate optimization variance, subset-resampling variance across different $40$-image draws is not captured in Table~\ref{tab:multiseed}.
  \item \textbf{Computational Footprint vs. Single-Stream CNNs:} Freezing DINOv2-Base adds $86.58\text{M}$ non-trainable parameters ($129.90\text{M}$ total parameters vs. $30.96\text{M}$ for ConvNeXt-Tiny+FPN), reducing inference throughput on a Turing T4 GPU from $91.9$~FPS to $17.4$~FPS. While $17.4$~FPS remains compatible with real-time endoscopic video withdrawal ($15\text{--}30$~FPS), edge deployment on embedded processors would benefit from knowledge distillation into a compact student.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic Nature of $S_3$ Corruptions and Cohort Dependencies:} Track~$S_3$ evaluates deterministic photometric and optical corruptions (NBI-like channel reweighting, gamma/vignette falloff, specular glare, and blur) applied to PolypGen-Dev frames rather than paired physical NBI/WLI clinical acquisitions. Furthermore, $S_2$ and $S_3$ reuse frames from PolypGen-Dev, ETIS, and ColonDB, and PolypGen-Extended retains $74$ frames sharing \texttt{C4} sequence prefixes with Dev; consequently, these 12 evaluation tracks represent diagnostic slices rather than 12 mutually disjoint patient cohorts, and positive mask filtering excludes negative-frame false-positive specificity analysis.
  \item \textbf{Conditional Prototype Supervision:} While all Round~9/10 arms share the exact same primary and deep-supervision segmentation loss Eq.~(\ref{eq:loss}), variants equipped with the Dual-Prototype head (\texttt{R9c}, \texttt{R9e}) additionally receive the $0.35\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{proto}}$ auxiliary supervision on $\pi_{\text{proto}}$. Thus, gains in \texttt{R9c}/\texttt{R9e} reflect the joint effect of the prototype module and its auxiliary supervision.
  \item \textbf{External Literature Baselines:} Our experimental suite focuses on controlled internal ablations (\texttt{R8a}--\texttt{R10e}) under identical deduplication and resolution ($352\times 352$) rather than re-training external architectures such as SAM-Med2D or UniverSeg under our $5\%$ few-shot split.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Ethical Compliance and Data Licensing:} No new patient data or human subjects were recruited for this study. All experiments were conducted exclusively on publicly available, de-identified benchmark datasets (Kvasir-SEG~\citep{jha2020kvasir}, PolypGen~\citep{ali2023polypgen}, CVC-ClinicDB, CVC-ColonDB~\citep{tajbakhsh2015automated}, CVC-300, and ETIS-LaribPolypDB~\citep{silva2014toward}) in compliance with their respective CC-BY / academic research licenses.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and Independent Cross-Harness Verification:} Full PyTorch source code (\texttt{src/dang/\{model,train,data,metrics,config\}.py}), deterministic deduplication manifests (\texttt{HYGIENE\_MANIFEST\_JSON}), per-image evaluation logs (\texttt{PER\_IMAGE\_CHUNK}), and figure generation scripts (\texttt{figs/generate\_paper\_figures.py}) are committed to the project repository. All raw log metrics, per-image aggregations ($584$ cohort checks), and methodological disclosures in this manuscript were independently audited and re-derived from raw run logs by both a \textbf{Claude Code (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, Anthropic)} agent and a \textbf{Codex (\texttt{gpt-6-astra}, OpenAI)} agent.

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented \textbf{DANG-Med}, a dual-stream attention-augmented and directional strip-aligned architecture for multi-center medical polyp segmentation. By uniting a 4-Scale Learned DPT Reassembler, a Dual-Prototype and Self-Support Margin Head, the Prototype-Anchored Spatial Support Envelope (PASSE), a Homomorphic Retinex Chromatic Stem with DINOv2-Conditioned Style Calibration, and an Uncertainty-Guided Directional Strip Decoder, DANG-Med achieves substantial, reproducible improvements across mDice, mIoU, Boundary F1, and HD95 on challenging few-shot, tiny/camouflaged, external OOD, and adverse endoscopic benchmarks.
